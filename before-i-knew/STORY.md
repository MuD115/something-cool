# قبل ما عرفت / Before I Knew

**An interactive story game set in besieged Ghouta, Syria — August 2014**

## Logline

In a besieged town in Ghouta, a man parts ways with his closest friend on an ordinary afternoon. Less than an hour later, he learns his friend is dead. *Before I Knew* is the story of that hour of unknowing, and the long night that follows — a night of grief, negotiation, visions, and the stubborn act of remaining human under siege.

---

## Characters

### سامي / Sami — *the player character*

Twenty-seven. Was studying civil engineering at Damascus University when the revolution began; never finished. Stayed in Ghouta when the siege closed. Now he maintains what's left of the town's water infrastructure — patching pipes, rigging hand pumps, running hoses between buildings. Quiet, observant, prone to the dark humour that siege life breeds. He uses practical work to hold himself together; when there's nothing to fix, the cracks show. His parents left Ghouta before the full siege tightened. He doesn't know if they're alive. He carries guilt about staying and guilt about what staying costs, and resolves neither.

**Inner life:** Sami thinks in textures — the weight of a wrench, the colour of rust on a valve, the specific silence after a barrel bomb lands. He avoids thinking about the future because the future requires hope, and hope feels reckless. Ahmad is the person who makes him laugh. Without that, he doesn't know what holds.

### أحمد / Ahmad — *the friend*

Twenty-eight. Arabic literature teacher. Even under siege, he kept teaching children in a basement room — grammar, poetry, a little maths when no one else would. Believed that keeping the ordinary rituals alive was itself a form of resistance. He kept a small journal, half lesson plans, half private writing. More openly warm than Sami, more willing to name what he felt. He called Sami "يا هندسة" (*ya handasa* — "hey, Engineering") as a joke that became a name.

**We know him only alive.** He appears in Act 1 and in flashbacks. His absence is what the rest of the story is built around.

### أبو يزن / Abu Yazan — *the neighbour*

Fifty-five. Owned a small grocery shop before the siege emptied it. Now he moves through the neighbourhood slowly, checking on people, carrying news. Lost his eldest son, Yazan, to a sniper in 2013. Speaks carefully, never wastes words. He delivers the news of Ahmad's death to Sami — not because anyone asked him to, but because he understands that how a person hears this matters.

**Inner life:** Abu Yazan long ago stopped asking why. He operates on a simpler question: who needs what, right now? His grief for his son is a room he enters and leaves; he does not perform it.

### أم أحمد / Um Ahmad — *Ahmad's mother*

Early fifties. A small, precise woman. Before the siege she was a seamstress. Now she patches everything — clothes, tarps, bandages. She appears in Act 2 or 3 depending on the player's path. Her grief is not dramatic; it is a withdrawal of light from her face. She does not cry in front of Sami. She asks him practical questions: Where was he? Was it quick? She needs these facts the way a surgeon needs a scalpel — to cut cleanly.

### الضابط ماهر / Officer Maher — *the regime officer*

Thirty-five. From a village near Tartous. Career military — not Ba'athist ideologue but a man from a poor family for whom the army was the only ladder. Has a wife, Rima, and two daughters, aged six and four. He has been posted at the siege perimeter for eight months. He is tired. He does not enjoy the shelling. He also does not stop it. His justification is the oldest one: if the regime falls, his family — Alawite, military-connected — will be killed. He may be right. He may be using that fear to avoid looking at what he is part of. In the negotiation scene, he is not a villain. He is a man standing on the other side of a line that history drew through both of them.

**Inner life:** Maher dreams about his daughters' school. He keeps a photo of them in his chest pocket. He rations his cigarettes. He has not spoken to his wife in eleven days because the signal is bad. He thinks about this more than he thinks about the people on the other side of his checkpoint.

### ليلى / Layla — *the child*

Ten. Collecting scrap metal from a collapsed building when Sami meets her. Bright, matter-of-fact in the way that siege children become. She isn't a symbol — she's a person whose childhood was replaced by arithmetic: how many kilos of metal for a bag of bulgur.

### The Cat

A thin tabby with a torn ear, sitting on rubble. It watches Sami. He watches it. No speech, no symbolism forced. Two creatures surviving in the same wreckage. It appears twice: once in Act 1, once in the night scenes. Its presence is a rest — a moment where nothing is asked of the player.

## Themes

1. **The hour of unknowing** — the cruel gap between a death and the moment you learn of it, when you were living as though the world was still whole.
2. **Ordinary life as resistance** — teaching children, fixing pipes, making bread from animal feed, charging a phone from a car battery. Siege life is not only horror; it is also Tuesday.
3. **Grief without ceremony** — when there is no safe place to bury, no time to mourn, no guarantee you will survive the funeral.
4. **Complicity and survival** — the regime officer is not a monster. The moral weight of the siege does not require him to be.
5. **Memory as habitation** — flashbacks are not just nostalgia; they are the rooms where the self still lives. Pre-revolution memories carry both warmth and the unspoken terror of the police state.
6. **Remaining** — the choice to stay, not as heroism, but as the thing you do when leaving would be a different kind of dying.

## Tools

The player acquires tools throughout the game. Each has practical gameplay use and thematic weight.

| Tool | Arabic | Acquired | Use |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Hand-crank flashlight** | كشّاف يدوي | Start of game | Illuminates dark interiors, basements, rubble. Requires periodic cranking (gameplay mechanic: hold button to crank, light fades if neglected). Essential for night scenes. |
| **Mirror shard** | شقفة مراية | Act 1, Scene 3 (found in rubble) | Peek around corners to check for sniper sight lines. Hold up at corner, reflects a limited view of the street ahead. |
| **Walkie-talkie** | لاسلكي | Act 1, Scene 3 (given by a spotter) | Receives warnings from spotters watching for helicopters and incoming fire. Crackles to life with alerts. Player must listen and react. Cannot transmit — receive only. |
| **Camera** | كاميرا | Act 2 (if witness path) or Act 3 | An old digital camera with a cracked screen. Used to document: aftermath of shelling, daily life, faces. Each photo is a choice — film costs nothing, but what you point it at defines what survives. Moral weight, not mechanical. |
| **White cloth** | قماشة بيضا | Act 2/3 (before negotiation) | A torn bedsheet. Carried on a stick to signal truce when approaching the regime checkpoint. Gameplay: equip it to enter the no-man's-land safely (or as safely as anything is). Without it, the player cannot approach. |
| **Lighter** | ولّاعة | Act 3 (found in Ahmad's belongings or given by Abu Yazan) | Light. Warmth. Burning a note that shouldn't be found. In the night scenes, used where the flashlight would draw too much attention — a small flame is less visible than a beam. |

## Acts

### Act 1: The Afternoon — *الضهرية*

**Time:** 3:00 PM – 4:15 PM, a hot August day.
**Duration:** 15–20 minutes of play.
**Status:** Full production script (see `script/act1.md`).

The act opens with Sami and Ahmad walking together through the damaged eastern quarter. They talk — not about the war, but around it. Ice cream they miss. A student of Ahmad's who asked what "abroad" means. The grape vine pushing through a collapsed wall. The walk introduces movement, the feel of the town, and the friendship.

They reach a junction. Ahmad is heading to check on a family in the southern quarter. They part casually — "see you tonight, God willing." Ahmad rounds a corner. The player watches the empty street.

The middle of the act is the hour of unknowing. Sami walks alone through the town. He encounters:
- An old man struggling with water jugs (Choice A: help or keep walking)
- A walkie-talkie warning of a jet (gameplay: go prone, wait)
- An airstrike on a distant block (environmental storytelling)
- Mortar shelling beginning (Choice B: shield nearby children or take cover)
- Children collecting scrap metal; Layla
- The stray cat, watching from rubble
- A flashback triggered by passing Ahmad's school: the town before the siege

The act ends with Scene 4: The News. Sami reaches a group of people gathered at a corner. Abu Yazan approaches. His face tells Sami before his words do. Ahmad was crossing a street in the southern quarter. A sniper. It was fast.

Choice C — the main branching point:
- **C1: "بدي شوفو" / "I need to see him"** → Sami moves toward the site. This opens the Retrieval path.
- **C2: "مين ساوى هيك" / "Who did this?"** → Sami seeks information, witnesses. This opens the Witness path.
- **C3: (silence)** → Sami sits down on the kerb. He cannot move. This opens the Grief path.

### Act 2: The Evening — *العصرية*

**Time:** 4:30 PM – 7:00 PM. The light turns gold, then amber, then fades.
**Duration:** 15–20 minutes of play.
**Status:** Full script in [`script/act2.md`](script/act2.md). Path A (Retrieval) is built and playable.

#### Path A — Retrieval (from C1)

Sami pushes toward the southern quarter. The streets are dangerous — the shelling hasn't stopped, and the sniper who killed Ahmad is still active. Gameplay: crouching along walls, using the mirror shard to check corners, listening to the walkie-talkie for helicopter warnings.

He reaches the area. Ahmad's body lies in a no-man's-land street between two frontlines. It cannot be reached without crossing into view of the regime checkpoint.

**Choice D:**
- **D1: Take the white cloth.** Sami finds a bedsheet, ties it to a metal rod. He will walk into the open and negotiate. → Leads to the negotiation with Officer Maher in Act 3.
- **D2: Try the back route.** A half-collapsed building offers a dangerous path — climbing through floors, crawling through rubble, risking structural collapse. → Gameplay-heavy sequence; physically reaches the body but at greater personal risk and without the moral encounter with the officer.

#### Path B — Witness (from C2)

Sami moves through the neighbourhood, finding people who saw what happened. He talks to witnesses, pieces together the last minutes of Ahmad's life. An older woman gives him Ahmad's camera — it was in his bag, thrown clear. Sami now has the camera.

**Choice E:**
- **E1: Use the camera.** Begin documenting — not just Ahmad's death but the siege itself. Photograph the aftermath, the people, the daily texture of survival. → The camera becomes a tool and a moral weight.
- **E2: Focus on the people.** Put the camera away. Sit with people. Listen. Record nothing but carry everything. → A quieter path; the emphasis shifts to human connection.

#### Path C — Grief (from C3)

Sami cannot act. Abu Yazan sits with him. Time passes. A flashback takes over: Sami and Ahmad at university, before the revolution. The memory of sitting in a lecture hall, passing notes, stepping outside into a Damascus street where the future was still abstract.

**Choice F:**
- **F1: Accept help.** When a neighbour offers to take him to Um Ahmad, Sami goes. He owes her that. → Scene with Um Ahmad; her questions force Sami out of paralysis.
- **F2: Walk alone.** Sami gets up and walks. No destination. Into the falling dark. → A solitary path; more vision sequences, more interior.

### Act 3: The Night — *الليل*

**Time:** 8:00 PM – 3:00 AM. Darkness. Moonlight through ruined rooftops.
**Duration:** 20–25 minutes of play.
**Status:** Full script in [`script/act3.md`](script/act3.md).

The longest act. The night paths share certain scenes — the moonlight walk, the cat's second appearance, the rooftop overlooking Damascus — but diverge in their central encounters.

#### The Negotiation (Retrieval path, D1 only)

Sami approaches the regime checkpoint under the white cloth. Two soldiers watch him. He is brought to Officer Maher.

The negotiation is a dialogue scene with limited but meaningful choices. Maher is cautious, not hostile. He calls Ahmad a "مسلح" (armed fighter). Sami says he was a teacher. Maher is unimpressed — everyone claims that. But something shifts when Sami describes Ahmad's students, their names, the poems he taught them. Maher has daughters.

**Negotiation approach (Choice G):**
- **G1: Dignity.** Sami remains calm, appeals to Maher's humanity. The conversation turns real. Maher admits he's tired. Sami doesn't absolve him. Maher lets Sami retrieve the body at dawn, under cover. → Leads to Ending 2.
- **G2: Anger.** Sami can't contain himself. He challenges Maher directly: you are starving children. Maher hardens. The negotiation nearly collapses but is rescued by a moment — Sami sees the photo of Maher's daughters fall from his pocket. A silence. → Partial success; body may or may not be retrieved.
- **G3: Pragmatic deal.** Sami offers something — information, a route, a concession. Morally compromising. Maher accepts quickly. The body is returned, but Sami is left with what he traded. → Leads to a variant of Ending 2, shadowed by cost.

#### The Dangerous Route (Retrieval path, D2)

A physical sequence. Sami climbs through a bombed building, crawls under collapsed floors, uses the flashlight in pitch darkness. The building groans. Gameplay: jumping, climbing, going prone through tight gaps. He reaches the street from above. The body is below. Getting it back is the challenge. This path is longer and more physically dangerous, but avoids the moral complexity of the negotiation.

#### The Night Walk (all paths)

Regardless of path, there is a sequence of walking through the town at night. The moonlight is bright — Ghouta's sky is unpolluted now; the power has been out for months. The streets are silver and black. Sami walks among ruined buildings, grapevines climbing through the wreckage. He encounters the cat again, sitting on a wall. They regard each other. The cat blinks. Sami walks on.

**Sound:** Crickets. Distant occasional gunfire. The creak of damaged structures. Sometimes silence.

#### The Visions

In the small hours, exhaustion and grief produce sequences that blur the real and unreal:
- **The classroom vision:** Sami enters Ahmad's basement classroom. It is full — every desk occupied. But the students are adults: people who have died during the siege. Ahmad is at the board, writing. He turns, and his face is light, and he says: "You're late, ya handasa." Sami blinks, and the room is empty, dusty, chairs overturned.
- **The orchard vision:** Sami walks through the grape orchards outside town. They are full and heavy with fruit. No damage, no war. The sound of water in an irrigation channel. He reaches for a grape and his hand passes through it.
- **The Damascus vision:** Sami is standing on a Damascus street — pre-war, alive, traffic and people and noise. He sees himself as a student, crossing the road. He tries to call out to his younger self. No sound comes.

These sequences use a distinct visual style (lighting shift, desaturated or hyper-saturated colour) and are non-interactive: the player walks through them but cannot change them. They are the mind's rebellion against the real.

#### The Flashbacks

Interspersed through the night:
- **Before the revolution:** A family gathering, probably Eid. The table is full. Someone tells a joke. Then a knock at the door — everyone freezes. It's only a neighbour. The relief. The unspoken: *this is how we lived, always listening for the knock.*
- **The first protest:** A crowd gathering after Friday prayers. Sami and Ahmad among them. The chanting starts. Fear and exhilaration. Then the sound of gunshots — far, then closer. The crowd scatters. Sami and Ahmad run. They hide behind a dumpster, breathing hard, looking at each other. Ahmad is grinning. "ما رح يرجعوا يسكتونا" — *They're never going to silence us again.*
- **The mukhabarat memory:** A childhood memory. Sami's father telling him, very quietly, never to repeat what is said at home. The portrait of Assad on the school wall. A classmate who disappeared. The teacher who said nothing. The knowledge that the walls listen.

#### The Rooftop

Near the end of Act 3, Sami climbs to a rooftop. He can see Damascus in the distance — lit up, glowing, alive. Ghouta is dark around him. The contrast is the siege made visible: the city that forgot them, or chose to, five kilometres away. The player can stay as long as they want. No timer, no objective. Just the view.

### Act 4: Dawn — *الفجر*

**Time:** 4:00 AM – 6:30 AM. The azan (call to prayer) sounds. First light.
**Duration:** 10–15 minutes of play.
**Status:** Full script in [`script/act4.md`](script/act4.md).

The shortest act. Dawn is resolution — not necessarily peace, but an ending to the night. The accumulated state variables determine which ending the player reaches.

The act opens with the fajr azan echoing across the town. Sami is wherever his path left him. The light changes — deep blue, then grey, then the first warm edge of orange on the eastern buildings. The sounds shift: birds begin (they never stopped, really, the siege didn't touch them). The day begins.

A final choice is offered in some paths, consolidating the player's arc. Then the ending plays.

## Endings

### Ending 1: الشاهد / The Witness

**Conditions:** Witness path (C2) + camera used extensively (E1) + documented throughout the night.

Dawn. Sami is on the rooftop, camera in hand. He has footage of everything — the crater, the children, the empty classroom, the grape vine, the moonlit streets. He reviews the last photo: it's of Ahmad's shoes, still on the street where he fell, placed neatly together by someone who cared.

Sami knows what the footage means. Not justice — not yet, maybe not ever. But a record. Proof that this happened, that these people were real, that the siege was not silence.

He packs the camera into a cloth bag. He's heard there are people who can get things out through the tunnels. He walks toward the edge of town, into the morning light. The camera bag is heavy on his shoulder.

**Final image:** Sami walking down a dusty road, the sun rising behind him, the town at his back. He does not look back.

**Final text:**
> شهادة واحدة ما بتغيّر شي. بس السكوت بيغيّر كل شي.
> *One testimony changes nothing. But silence changes everything.*

### Ending 2: الوداع الأخير / The Last Farewell

**Conditions:** Retrieval path (C1) + negotiation successful (D1, dignity or pragmatic approach) + body retrieved.

Dawn. The retrieval happened in the grey hour before sunrise — Sami crossing the no-man's-land under Maher's wary gaze, a stretcher improvised from a door, two neighbours helping. Now Ahmad is wrapped in a white sheet in the small cemetery between the grape arbours on the eastern edge of town.

Um Ahmad is there. Abu Yazan. A handful of others. There is no imam — he was killed months ago — so Abu Yazan recites what he remembers. Sami reads a passage from Ahmad's journal: a poem Ahmad had copied out, by Mahmoud Darwish, with Ahmad's note in the margin: "لطلابي" — *for my students*.

A bird sings in the arbour overhead. The grapes are not yet ripe. They will ripen, and no one will pick them, and they will fall.

**Final image:** The small mound of earth, the white stones placed on it, the grape vine arching over it. Sami's hand resting on the earth for a moment before he stands.

**Final text:**
> ما قدرت قلّو وداعاً. بس قدرت جبتو لبيتو.
> *I couldn't say goodbye to him. But I could bring him home.*

### Ending 3: النفق / The Tunnel

**Conditions:** Grief path (C3, F2) or accumulated despair from failed actions + low compassion/courage state.

Dawn. Sami is at the edge of town, near the entrance to the smuggling tunnels. He has been walking all night with no destination, and his feet brought him here. The tunnel mouth is a hole in a basement wall, propped with timber, disappearing into blackness. People use it to move supplies in. Some use it to leave.

He stands at the entrance. Behind him, the town is waking up. Somewhere a generator starts. A child calls out. Ahead: the tunnel, and whatever is on the other side. Damascus, maybe. Or further. Somewhere that isn't here.

He steps into the dark.

**Final image:** The tunnel entrance, the morning light behind Sami shrinking to a bright rectangle, then a sliver, then gone.

**Final text:**
> في ناس بتبقى وفي ناس بتمشي. مش كل مشي هروب. ومش كل بقاء شجاعة.
> *Some people stay and some people walk. Not every departure is flight. Not every staying is courage.*

### Ending 4: الباقي / The One Who Remains

**Conditions:** High compassion (helped old man, shielded children) + grief path with acceptance (F1) or witness path focusing on people (E2) + visited Um Ahmad.

Dawn. Sami is in Ahmad's basement classroom. He arrived here without deciding to — his night walk ended at this door, and he went in. The room is as Ahmad left it: a blackboard with yesterday's lesson still on it (a conjugation table), a stack of exercise books, a jar of chalk. The chairs are small, child-sized.

He hears footsteps upstairs. Then on the stairs. A child appears in the doorway — one of Ahmad's students, a boy of eight or nine, holding a school bag made from a rice sack. He looks at Sami.

**The boy:**
> أستاز أحمد وينو؟
> *Where is teacher Ahmad?*

Sami looks at the blackboard. At the chalk. At the boy.

He picks up a piece of chalk. He writes the date on the board: ١٤ آب ٢٠١٤.

**Final image:** Sami at the blackboard, chalk in hand, turning toward the boy. Ahmad's journal is open on the desk. Through the small basement window, the morning light falls in a rectangle on the floor.

**Final text:**
> الحياة مش بس إنك تضل عايش. الحياة إنك تضل إنسان.
> *Life isn't just staying alive. Life is staying human.*

### Ending 5: ساعة الذئب / The Wolf's Hour

**Conditions:** Failed negotiation (anger approach) OR dangerous route with heavy cost OR prolonged isolation (F2) with low compassion + no human connection made through the night.

Dawn. Sami is sitting in the rubble of a building — maybe the one where he took the dangerous route, maybe one he found in his wandering. He is not injured, but something in him has gone quiet. The kind of quiet that isn't peace.

The cat appears. It picks its way over the broken concrete and sits near him — not touching, not asking, just present. They sit together as the light changes.

The fajr azan sounds from somewhere — someone, still, making the call. It echoes off the damaged buildings and fades.

Sami doesn't move. He will, eventually. But not yet.

**Final image:** Sami and the cat, side by side in the rubble, the dawn light reaching them from the east. The town is waking around them — sounds of life, fragile but persistent. Sami's eyes are open, looking at nothing in particular.

**Final text:**
> ما خلصت القصة. بس خلص الليل.
> *The story didn't end. But the night did.*

## State Variables

| Variable | Type | Set by | Effect |
|---|---|---|---|
| `compassion` | int (0–3) | +1 for each: helping old man with water (A1), shielding children (B1), kind dialogue choices | Influences ending selection. ≥ 2 enables Ending 4. |
| `courage` | int (0–3) | +1 for each: rushing to site (C1), taking the dangerous route (D2), confronting the officer directly | Influences scene variants and officer's respect. |
| `grief_response` | enum: `action`, `witness`, `withdrawal` | Set by Choice C (C1→action, C2→witness, C3→withdrawal) | Determines Act 2 path and base emotional register. |
| `path` | enum: `retrieval`, `witness`, `grief` | Set by Choice C, refined by Choices D/E/F | Primary story branch. |
| `negotiation_outcome` | enum: `success`, `partial`, `failure`, `skipped` | Set by Choice G and accumulated state | Whether the body can be retrieved via negotiation. |
| `documented` | bool | Set true if camera is used in multiple scenes | Required for Ending 1. |
| `children_helped` | bool | Set by Choice B1 | Contributes to compassion; affects Layla's dialogue. |
| `body_retrieved` | bool | Set by successful negotiation or dangerous route completion | Required for Ending 2. |
| `um_khaled_visited` | bool | Set if player visits Um Ahmad in Act 2 or 3 | Affects ending scenes; opens dialogue in Ending 2. |
| `officer_connection` | bool | Set if dignity path in negotiation; Maher opens up | Affects Maher's final gesture; enables dawn retrieval. |
| `khaled_journal` | bool | Set if player finds and keeps Ahmad's journal | Appears in endings; provides the poem for Ending 2. |
| `isolation` | int (0–3) | +1 for: refusing help (A2), taking cover alone (B2), walking alone (F2), avoiding people through the night | High isolation + low compassion → Ending 5 or 3. |

**Ending selection logic (simplified):**

```
if documented AND path == witness:
    → Ending 1 (The Witness)
elif body_retrieved AND (negotiation_outcome == success OR dangerous_route_complete):
    → Ending 2 (The Farewell)
elif isolation >= 2 AND compassion <= 1:
    if courage <= 1:
        → Ending 3 (The Tunnel)
    else:
        → Ending 5 (The Wolf's Hour)
elif compassion >= 2 AND (um_khaled_visited OR children_helped):
    → Ending 4 (The One Who Remains)
else:
    → Ending 5 (The Wolf's Hour)  # default/fallback
```

## Additional Suggested Moments and Encounters

These can be woven into Acts 2–4 depending on path and pacing:

1. **The well run** — Sami helps carry water from a communal well through exposed streets. Gameplay: crouching with heavy jugs, slower movement, choosing the safest route. The well is in a courtyard; people queue in silence, watching the sky.

2. **The baker** — A man grinding animal feed into flour with a hand mill, baking bread in a clay oven improvised from rubble. He offers Sami a piece. The bread is grey and gritty. It's the best thing Sami has ever tasted. Dialogue: the baker talks about what flour used to be.

3. **The grape harvest** — A woman picking grapes from a vine growing over a destroyed building. She fills a basket. She doesn't look up at the sound of distant shelling; she's calibrated to which sounds mean danger. The grapes are small and sour from neglect but still alive.

4. **The field hospital** — Sami passes the overwhelmed medical point. He doesn't go in. Through the door: voices, a generator, the smell of disinfectant mixed with blood. A medic steps out for air, leans against the wall, smokes half a cigarette, goes back in.

5. **The satellite dish** — Climbing to a rooftop to catch a phone signal. Sami tries to call his parents. The call connects for three seconds: his mother's voice, mid-sentence, then static. He tries again. Nothing. He stands there holding the phone to his ear long after the signal dies.

6. **The wedding** — Distant sound of music and clapping. Someone in the neighbourhood is getting married, in a basement, at night, during a siege. Life insisting on itself. Sami can listen from outside. If he enters, he sees people dancing, a bride in a white dress made from curtain material, genuine joy that exists alongside everything else.

7. **The chess game** — Two old men playing chess in a courtyard by candlelight, as though the war is an inconvenience interrupting their afternoon. One of them is missing a rook; they've replaced it with a bullet casing. They don't acknowledge Sami. Interaction text: "They've been playing this game for six days."

8. **The generator repair** — Sami's engineering skills are needed. A small generator that powers a medical fridge has broken down. Gameplay: a short puzzle sequence with mechanical interaction. Success keeps the medicine cold. The gratitude is quiet — a nod, a glass of tea.

9. **The graffiti wall** — A wall covered in revolutionary art and children's drawings. Flowers, flags, stick figures, helicopters. One drawing shows a house with a family inside, surrounded by fire, but the family is smiling. Below it, in a child's handwriting: "بكرا أحلى" — *Tomorrow is more beautiful.*

10. **The night garden** — A small vegetable garden someone has kept alive in a courtyard. Tomatoes, mint, a struggling courgette. Watered at night when there's less risk. Sami can water it (interaction). The ordinariness of tending a garden during a siege.

11. **The record player** — In a damaged apartment, an old record player with a cracked record. Sami can try to play it. It produces a warped, ghostly version of a Fairuz song. The sound fills the empty room.

12. **The sniper's view** — A moment where Sami, climbing through a building, finds himself at a high window overlooking the no-man's-land. He can see the regime checkpoint. He can see the street where Ahmad fell. For a moment, he sees what the sniper saw. The vertigo of that perspective.

## Content Note

*Before I Knew* depicts life under military siege, including artillery bombardment, sniper fire, starvation, and the death of a civilian. It contains no graphic depictions of violence or gore — the friend's body is always covered or off-screen. The game does depict the psychological effects of siege: grief, fear, moral injury, hallucination from exhaustion. It includes a conversation with a regime officer that presents his perspective without endorsing it; the moral weight of what the Assad regime did to besieged civilian areas is conveyed through the texture of daily life rather than through explicit condemnation.

The story is set in Ghouta during the Syrian civil war and draws on documented accounts of siege conditions. It aims for truthfulness, not sensationalism. Player choices carry moral weight but the game does not lecture; it trusts the player to feel what the details make visible.

**Age recommendation:** 16+
**Content warnings:** War, siege, death of a loved one, grief, military violence (heard/implied, not shown), food scarcity, psychological distress.
