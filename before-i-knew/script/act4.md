# Act 4: Dawn — الفجر

**Full production script — 10–15 minutes of play**
**Time:** 4:00 AM – 6:30 AM, 15 August 2014
**Setting:** The besieged town — various locations depending on path and accumulated state

*The shortest act. The night is over. What remains is determined by everything the player has done — every choice, every hesitation, every act of kindness or withdrawal. The state variables converge here into one of five endings. Dawn is not resolution. It is the next thing that happens.*

---

## TITLE CARD

> الفجر.
> *Dawn.*

> الليل أطول شي بالحصار. بس بيخلص.
> *The night is the longest thing in the siege. But it ends.*

---

## PART 1: THE DESCENT — النزول

*[ALL PATHS — the opening of Act 4]*

### Scene 4A-1: Leaving the Rooftop

**Location:** The rooftop from Act 3, then the stairwell down
**Time:** 4:05 AM
**Lighting:** The eastern horizon is a band of deep indigo lightening toward grey-blue. The stars are still visible overhead but fading at the edges. Damascus's lights to the west are dimmer now — street by street, the city turns off as its people sleep. Ghouta is the same dark it always is, but the quality of dark is changing: less absolute, more permeable. The buildings are beginning to have edges again.
**Ambient sound:** The fajr azan continues — a second voice has joined from another quarter, slightly behind, creating a staggered echo. Birds — tentative, then more. Crickets fading. The world shifting registers.

*Sami stands. He has been sitting on the rooftop for some time — minutes, maybe longer. The azan called him back from wherever he was. He looks east, toward the light that isn't yet light but the promise of it.*

*[ACTING NOTE: This is the hinge. Everything that follows depends on what kind of night Sami has had — what he did, whom he saw, what he chose. The player doesn't know which ending is coming. Sami doesn't know what the morning will ask of him. He only knows the night is ending, and he survived it.]*

**[GAMEPLAY — DESCENT]**

*Sami crosses the rooftop and enters the stairwell. The flashlight is unnecessary now — the first grey light seeps through gaps in the walls, through windows with no glass, through holes where shells entered. He descends six floors. His footsteps echo in the concrete stairwell. Each floor is a layer of the building's life: a door ajar showing a kitchen with pots still on the stove; a child's bicycle on its side on a landing; a balcony with laundry still hanging, bleached and frayed by months of weather.*

*He reaches the ground floor and steps into the street.*

### Scene 4A-2: The Dawn Street

**Location:** The eastern quarter — a main residential street
**Time:** 4:15 AM
**Lighting:** Pre-dawn. The sky is the colour of a bruise healing — deep blue yielding to grey, with the faintest edge of warm colour at the eastern horizon, behind the ruined buildings. The buildings themselves are silhouettes becoming shapes becoming structures. Details emerge: a doorframe, a vine, a crater, a wall pocked with bullet holes. Everything is desaturated — the world is greyscale with a suggestion of colour seeping in from the east.
**Ambient sound:** Birds — more now. A rooster from somewhere improbable. The azan fading. Footsteps — not Sami's. Other people are stirring. A door opens and closes. A woman's voice calling a name. The town is waking.

*[ACTING NOTE: Dawn in a siege town is not triumphant. It is tentative. Every morning is a question: did we survive the night? The answer comes slowly, in the sounds of people moving, doors opening, the ordinary business of being alive resuming. Each new morning is both a relief and a reset: the siege continues, the shelling will resume, the hunger will return. But for this hour — this pale, in-between hour — the town breathes.]*

**[INTERACTION — MORNING]**

> **Arabic:** أول ضو. الصبح بالحصار إلو طعم — مش فرح ومش حزن. بس إنك لسا هون. لسا عم تتنفس. هاد بيكفي ليكون بداية.
> **English:** First light. Morning in the siege has a taste — not happiness, not sadness. Just that you're still here. Still breathing. That's enough to be a beginning.

*Sami walks through the waking street. The pace is slow — not the purposeful stride of Act 2, not the careful navigation of Act 3. A walk without destination. His body decides where to go.*

**[ENVIRONMENTAL STORYTELLING]**

*As Sami walks, the town reveals its morning:*

- *A man carries a yellow jerrycan of water on his shoulder, walking quickly, hugging the eastern wall where the buildings cast shadow. He nods at Sami.*
- *An older woman sweeps the threshold of her damaged building with a straw broom. Half the building is rubble. She sweeps the half that stands.*
- *Two boys, maybe twelve, emerge from a basement carrying textbooks — exercise books, really, handmade, stitched from loose paper. They're heading to a lesson. They don't know yet that their teacher is dead.*

*[ACTING NOTE: The two boys heading to Ahmad's class — this is not a dramatic reveal. They pass Sami, and he sees them, and he says nothing. He might not even connect it immediately. The player will connect it. The boys walk on, heading toward a classroom that will be empty. This is the weight of the act: the aftermath radiates outward from the death, touching lives that don't yet know they've been touched.]*

## PART 2: THE FINAL CHOICE — الخيار الأخير

*[CONDITIONAL — appears only for certain path/state combinations]*

### Scene 4B-1: The Junction

**Location:** The junction where the eastern quarter meets the road toward the southern quarter — the same junction where Sami and Ahmad parted in Act 1
**Time:** 4:25 AM
**Lighting:** Stronger now. The east is a band of amber beneath the grey. The buildings have colour again — the ochre of sandstone, the grey-green of surviving foliage, the rust of exposed rebar. The street where Ahmad walked away is visible, empty, ordinary.

*Sami reaches the junction. He stops. This is where they parted. "See you tonight, God willing." Ahmad rounded that corner. Sami kept walking.*

*[ACTING NOTE: The player has been here before — in Act 1, Scene 2. The junction should feel both familiar and transformed. The same geometry, the same corner. But the light is different, the time is different, and Ahmad is absent in a way that has changed the meaning of the space itself. A place is not just coordinates — it's what happened there.]*

**[INTERACTION — THE JUNCTION]**

> **Arabic:** هون افترقنا. "بشوفك بالليل إن شاء الله." أربعتعشر ساعة من وقتها. أطول أربعتعشر ساعة بحياتي.
> **English:** This is where we parted. "See you tonight, God willing." Fourteen hours since then. The longest fourteen hours of my life.

**H2: Walk toward the edge of town — toward the tunnels.**

> **English:** The classroom. Ahmad's classroom. I don't know why — my feet are taking me there. Something is waiting, or I am.

*[Sets compassion +1. Pushes toward Ending 4.]*

## ENDING 1: الشاهد / THE WITNESS

**Conditions:** `documented == true AND path == witness`

### Scene E1-1: The Last Frame

**Location:** The rooftop (if Sami remained) or a high point in the eastern quarter
**Time:** 4:30 AM
**Lighting:** The sky has opened. The horizon is a line of molten gold beneath grey-blue. The first direct sunlight hasn't reached the town yet — it's still below the buildings to the east — but the quality of light has changed: everything is visible, everything is lit from within, as though the air itself is luminous. The damaged buildings, in this light, look almost beautiful. Almost.
**Ambient sound:** Birds in full voice now. A generator starting somewhere. The sound of water — someone filling a container from a tap or hose. The town is awake.

*Sami sits on the rooftop edge, feet hanging over the parapet, the camera in his hands. The cracked screen shows the last photograph he took: the view from this rooftop at night, Damascus lit, Ghouta dark. He scrolls back through the photos he's taken through the night and evening.*

**[GAMEPLAY — PHOTO REVIEW]**

*The player scrolls through the camera's memory. Each photo fills the screen for a moment — a slideshow of what Sami chose to document:*

1. *The crater where the barrel bomb fell — rubble, dust, a shoe.*
2. *Layla and the children, sorting scrap metal in the afternoon light.*
3. *The field hospital — the medic leaning against the wall, smoking.*
4. *The baker's hands, grey with animal-feed flour.*
5. *People gathered at the corner where the news arrived — Abu Yazan's back, turned toward Sami.*
6. *The no-man's-land street in last light — empty, still.*
7. *The moonlit grape vine growing through the collapsed building.*
8. *The night garden — tomatoes, mint, a struggling courgette.*
9. *The mosque's cracked minaret against the stars.*
10. *The graffiti wall — "بكرا أحلى" in a child's handwriting.*

*The last photo: **Ahmad's shoes**. Two leather shoes, cracked and dusty, placed neatly together on the pavement by someone who cared. They sit at the edge of the frame, at the edge of the no-man's-land, in the last light of the previous day.*

*[ACTING NOTE: The shoes are not a symbol. They are specific objects that belonged to a specific person. Someone — perhaps Um Said, the woman who covered his body — placed them together, a small act of care in the middle of a sniper zone. The detail is not invented; in many documented cases of siege deaths, bystanders who could not safely retrieve a body would at least arrange its belongings. The shoes are what survive.]*

**[INTERACTION — THE PHOTOS]**

> **Arabic:** مية وثلاث صور. مية وثلاث لحظة ضلّت. كل صورة بتقول: هاد صار. هدول ناس كانوا هون. هاد مكان كان عامر.
> **English:** A hundred and three photos. A hundred and three moments that lasted. Each one says: this happened. These people were here. This place was alive.

### Scene E1-2: The Decision

**Time:** 4:40 AM
**Lighting:** The sun is about to clear the eastern buildings. A single beam of direct light cuts across a distant rooftop, blazing orange against the grey.

*Sami wraps the camera in a cloth — carefully, deliberately, the way you wrap something that matters. He puts it in a shoulder bag. He stands.*

*He's heard there are people who can move things through the tunnels — not the smuggling tunnels for food, but the documentation networks. People who carry footage, testimonies, evidence. They send it to journalists outside, to archives, to anyone who will look. Most of it disappears into the noise. Some of it doesn't.*

**[INTERIOR MONOLOGUE — TEXT ON SCREEN]**

> **Arabic:** ما رح تجيب عدالة. هالصور ما رح ترجّع أحمد ولا توقف الحصار ولا تخلّي العالم يهتم. بس إذا ضاعوا — إذا ما حدا شاف — بيصير كإنو ما صار شي. وصار. كل شي صار.
> **English:** It won't bring justice. These photos won't bring Ahmad back or end the siege or make the world care. But if they're lost — if no one sees — it's as though nothing happened. And it did. All of it happened.

### Scene E1-3: The Walk

**Location:** Streets leading toward the eastern edge of town
**Time:** 4:50 AM
**Lighting:** Full pre-sunrise glow. The sky is light, the sun not yet visible but its warmth already touching the upper floors of buildings. The streets are golden-grey. Shadows are long but thinning.
**Ambient sound:** The town's morning sounds — fuller now. A child's voice. A door. Footsteps. The sound of something cooking — someone has found something to cook.

*Sami descends from the rooftop and walks through the town toward its eastern edge. The camera bag hangs from his shoulder. The walk is steady, unhurried — not fleeing, not heroic. A man carrying something that needs to get out.*

**[GAMEPLAY — FINAL WALK]**

*The player walks Sami through the waking streets. No obstacles, no choices, no danger. The walk is the point. The town passes on either side: damaged, functioning, alive. People nod at Sami. He nods back.*

*He passes the grape vine — the one from Act 1, growing through the collapsed building. In the dawn light, the leaves are bright green. The grapes are small, hard, not yet ripe. They will ripen in September, if the vine survives, if anyone is left to pick them.*

*He passes the corner where Abu Yazan told him. The corner is empty now. An ordinary corner.*

*He passes the junction where he and Ahmad parted. The street where Ahmad walked away is lit by the first direct sunlight.*

**[INTERACTION — THE JUNCTION]**

> **Arabic:** هون كان آخر مرة شفتو. ما كنت عارف. ما كنت عارف إنو عم ودّعو.
> **English:** This is where I last saw him. I didn't know. I didn't know I was saying goodbye.

*Sami keeps walking. He reaches the edge of town — the last buildings, then the open land, the orchards, the fields. The road stretches east, dusty, pitted, the sun now cresting the horizon directly ahead.*

### Scene E1-4: The Horizon

**Location:** The eastern edge of town — a dusty road between the last buildings and the orchards
**Time:** 5:05 AM
**Lighting:** Sunrise. The sun clears the horizon — a disc of white-gold fire, enormous, close. The road is lit from directly ahead. Sami walks into the light. His shadow stretches behind him, long, tapering back toward the town.
**Ambient sound:** Wind in the grape leaves. Birds. The town's sounds receding behind him. Ahead: silence, open land, the sun.

*[ACTING NOTE: He does not look back. Not because he's leaving forever — he might return, he probably will, the tunnels go both ways. He doesn't look back because looking back would break the motion, and right now the motion is all he has. Forward. One foot, then the other. The camera on his shoulder. The evidence that this happened.]*

**[GAMEPLAY — FINAL MOVEMENT]**

*The player controls Sami walking east along the road. The camera angle slowly pulls back — Sami becomes smaller, the road longer, the town behind him a silhouette against the brightening sky. The player can keep walking until the figure is small against the landscape.*

*The screen holds on this image: a man on a road, a bag on his shoulder, the sun ahead, the town behind.*

**[FADE — slow, over ten seconds]**

**[FINAL TEXT — white on black]**

> شهادة واحدة ما بتغيّر شي.
> بس السكوت بيغيّر كل شي.

> *One testimony changes nothing.*
> *But silence changes everything.*

**[HOLD — five seconds]**

**[FADE TO BLACK]**

## ENDING 2: الوداع الأخير / THE LAST FAREWELL

**Conditions:** `body_retrieved == true AND (negotiation_outcome == success OR negotiation_outcome == partial)`

### Scene E2-1: The Retrieval

**Location:** The no-man's-land street in the southern quarter
**Time:** 4:20 AM — the grey hour, neither night nor day
**Lighting:** The palest possible light — the sky is lighter than the ground. Objects have no shadow yet; everything is flat, dimensionless, grey. The no-man's-land is sixty metres of exposed street, buildings on either side, the regime checkpoint at one end. In this light, the street looks almost peaceful. It is not.
**Ambient sound:** Near-silence. The occasional radio crackle from the checkpoint. Wind. The sound of Sami's own breathing, loud in his ears. Footsteps — his, and the two men behind him.

*[This scene plays only if negotiation_outcome == success (G1 dignity path or G3 pragmatic deal). If G2 anger path with partial success, a variant plays — see E2-1b below.]*

*Sami stands at the edge of the no-man's-land. Beside him: Abu Yazan and a younger man — a neighbour, Raed, who volunteered. They have an improvised stretcher: a door taken from a damaged building, two rope handles tied through the hinge-holes.*

*The white cloth hangs from a metal rod, held by Raed. In the grey light, it is the brightest thing in the scene.*

*Across the street, at the checkpoint: two soldiers watch. Officer Maher stands behind them. He gave the window — twenty minutes, starting now. No weapons visible, no sudden movements, direct path only.*

**[GAMEPLAY — THE CROSSING]**

*The player walks Sami into the no-man's-land. The pace is controlled — slow, deliberate, the stretcher balanced between the three men. The crossing takes ninety seconds of real time. No music. No interface elements. Just the walk.*

*Halfway across, a sound: a **radio crackle** from the checkpoint. One of the soldiers raises a hand. Everyone freezes. The soldier speaks into the radio — inaudible from this distance. Then lowers his hand. The moment passes.*

*They reach Ahmad's body. It is where it fell — but not as it fell. Someone covered it hours ago: a white sheet, weighted with stones at the corners. Um Said's work. Ahmad's shoes sit beside the sheet, placed together.*

*[ACTING NOTE: The body is covered. It stays covered. The player never sees Ahmad's wound, his face, any detail of how he died. What the player sees: a white sheet in the grey street, a pair of shoes, and the shape beneath — a shape that is unmistakably a person, unmistakably still.]*

*The three men lift the body onto the stretcher-door. The weight is real — Sami staggers slightly. Abu Yazan steadies the other end. Raed walks ahead with the cloth.*

*They carry Ahmad back across the no-man's-land. Ninety seconds. The checkpoint watches.*

*[ACTING NOTE ON OFFICER MAHER: If the player took the dignity path (G1), Maher watches the crossing without expression. As Sami passes the midpoint, returning, Maher does a small thing: he turns slightly away. Not looking away from guilt — looking away to give them privacy. It's the smallest possible gesture. It means everything or nothing.]*

*[If pragmatic deal (G3): Maher watches the entire crossing without moving. His face is closed. The deal was transactional. There is no gesture. The body is retrieved, but the air between them is heavy with what was exchanged.]*

### Scene E2-1b: The Retrieval — Anger Variant

*[Plays if negotiation_outcome == partial (G2 anger path, where the confrontation nearly collapsed but was rescued by the photograph moment).]*

*The retrieval is the same, but with differences:*

- *Only Sami and one helper cross — Maher allowed the retrieval but set conditions: two people maximum, five minutes.*
- *The soldiers are tenser. One keeps his rifle raised, not aimed but present.*
- *The crossing is faster, more urgent. The player must move without stopping.*
- *Maher does not turn away. He watches every step. His face is unreadable.*

*The body is retrieved. But the cost — the confrontation, the things Sami said, Maher's hardening — hangs in the air.*

### Scene E2-2: The Burial

**Location:** The small cemetery in the grape arbours, eastern edge of town
**Time:** 5:15 AM
**Lighting:** The sun is up. Golden light filters through the grape leaves, dappling the ground. The cemetery is small — a dozen graves, some old, some new. The new ones have fresher earth, simpler markers. The grape vines arch overhead, providing shade. The grapes are hard, green, unripe.
**Ambient sound:** Birds in the vines. Wind through leaves. Footsteps on packed earth. The sound of a shovel — two neighbours dug the grave during the night, while Sami was on the rooftop.

*A small group is gathered: Um Ahmad, silent, a black headscarf tight around her face. Abu Yazan, standing with his hands clasped. Raed. Two other neighbours. The baker from Act 2, still dusted with flour. Um Said, the woman who covered the body.*

*[ACTING NOTE: The group is small because it has to be. Funerals in the siege are quick — gathering people in the open is dangerous, and there is no imam (he was killed months ago). This is not ceremony. It is necessity with what dignity can be salvaged.]*

*The body — still wrapped in the white sheet — is lowered into the grave. The men help. The earth goes back in. It takes four minutes. No one speaks during the filling.*

*Abu Yazan steps forward. He recites what he remembers of the janazah prayer — his voice is rough, unpractised, missing words. He pauses, closes his eyes, finds the next phrase. No one corrects him. No one needs to.*

**أبو يزن:**
> *(reciting, rough, pausing between phrases)* اللهم اغفرلو وارحمو... وعافيه واعف عنو... اللهم أكرم نزلو... ووسّع مدخلو...
> *(reciting, rough, pausing between phrases)* O God, forgive him and have mercy on him... pardon him and grant him well-being... honour the place where he arrives... and make his entrance wide...

*He trails off. He has reached the limit of what he remembers. A silence.*

**أبو يزن:**
> *(quietly, to Sami)* إذا بدّك تقول شي.
> *(quietly, to Sami)* If you want to say something.

### Scene E2-3: Ahmad's Journal

*[Plays if ahmad_journal == true — Sami has Ahmad's journal from Abu Yazan in Act 3.]*

*Sami takes the journal from his jacket. He opens it to a page near the back — a page he found during the night, marked with a fold. It's a poem, not Ahmad's own but one he copied out. Mahmoud Darwish. In the margin, in Ahmad's handwriting: "لطلابي" — for my students.*

*Sami reads. His voice is steady at first, then not.*

**سامي:**
> *(reading from the journal)* "على هذي الأرض ما يستحق الحياة..."
> *(reading from the journal)* "On this earth, there is that which deserves life..."

> "تردّد أبريل، رائحة الخبز في الفجر، آراء امرأة في الرجال..."
> "April's hesitation, the smell of bread at dawn, a woman's opinion of men..."

> "كتابات أسخيلوس، أول الحب..."
> "The writings of Aeschylus, the beginning of love..."

> "عشب على حجر... أمهات تقفن على خيط ناي..."
> "Grass on a stone... mothers standing on a thread of flute-song..."

> "والخوف من ذكريات..."
> "And the fear of memories..."

*His voice breaks. He closes the journal. A bird sings in the grape vine overhead. The silence holds.*

### Scene E2-3b: Without the Journal

*[Plays if ahmad_journal == false.]*

*Sami has nothing prepared. He stands at the graveside. The group waits. He opens his mouth and closes it. Then:*

**سامي:**
> *(simply)* كان أحسن واحد فينا. مش لأنو ما كان يخاف — كان يخاف متل ما كلنا منخاف. بس كان يضل يعلّم. كل يوم ينزل عالقبو ويفتح الدفاتر ويعلّم. هاد يلي كان.
> *(simply)* He was the best of us. Not because he wasn't afraid — he was afraid like we're all afraid. But he kept teaching. Every day he'd go down to the basement and open the exercise books and teach. That's who he was.

*Um Ahmad makes a sound — not a cry, not a word. A sound from beneath words. She bends and places her hand on the fresh earth, flat, pressing down, as if to keep something in or to feel something still there.*

### Scene E2-4: The Grapes

**Time:** 5:30 AM

*The burial is over. People disperse — slowly, reluctantly, the way people leave a place they're not ready to leave. Um Ahmad is the last. Abu Yazan walks with her, one hand under her elbow, guiding her through the cemetery gate.*

*Sami stays. He stands over the grave. The white stones have been placed — one at the head, one at the foot. The grape vine arches overhead. A cluster of grapes hangs directly above the grave, small, hard, green.*

**[INTERACTION — THE GRAPES]**

> **Arabic:** العنب لسا أخضر. بيستوي بأيلول — بعد أسبوعين، تلاتة. إذا ضل حدا يقطفو. بيستوي فوق قبرو وبينزل وما حدا بياكلو وبيرجع تراب. أو بيجي حدا ويقطفو ويحطّو بصحن ويقول بسم الله. ما بعرف أيّا أحلى.
> **English:** The grapes are still green. They'll ripen in September — two weeks, three. If anyone's left to pick them. They'll ripen over his grave and fall and no one will eat them and they'll become earth again. Or someone will come and pick them and put them on a plate and say bismillah. I don't know which is more beautiful.

*Sami kneels. He places his hand on the earth — the same gesture as Um Ahmad, flat, pressing.*

**[FINAL IMAGE — slow zoom out]**

*The camera pulls back slowly. Sami kneeling, his hand on the earth. The white stones. The grape vine, its leaves catching the morning light, the small hard clusters of green fruit hanging in the golden air. The cemetery wall. The orchards beyond. The town, damaged, standing, smoking faintly, alive.*

*Sami's hand lifts from the earth. He stands. He doesn't wipe his eyes. He turns and walks through the cemetery gate, toward the town, toward whatever the day requires.*

**[FADE — slow, over ten seconds]**

**[FINAL TEXT — white on black]**

> ما قدرت قلّو وداعاً.
> بس قدرت جبتو لبيتو.

> *I couldn't say goodbye to him.*
> *But I could bring him home.*

**[HOLD — five seconds]**

**[FADE TO BLACK]**

## ENDING 3: النفق / THE TUNNEL

**Conditions:** `(path == grief AND isolation >= 2 AND compassion <= 1) OR (Choice H == H2)`

### Scene E3-1: The Edge

**Location:** The eastern edge of town — the last inhabited buildings before the orchards and open land
**Time:** 4:35 AM
**Lighting:** The pre-dawn glow reaches even here, where the town thins into agricultural land. The last buildings are farm structures — stone walls, flat roofs, some collapsed, some standing. Beyond: the orchards, the fields, and then the open land where the tunnels run. The light is grey-blue, cool, without warmth yet.
**Ambient sound:** Wind across open ground — a different sound than the wind in streets. Wider, emptier. A dog far off. The generator sound from the town is barely audible. Here, at the edge, the siege feels like a membrane: thin, permeable, almost possible to pass through.

*Sami has been walking for hours. Not toward anything — away from. The town behind him is a geometry of damage and persistence, people he didn't speak to, doors he didn't open, hands he didn't take. Abu Yazan offered to sit with him and he walked. A neighbour offered food and he walked. The night passed in motion, the visions and flashbacks falling on him like weather, and he kept walking, and his feet brought him here.*

*[ACTING NOTE: This is the accumulation of isolation. Every choice the player made to turn away, to keep walking, to take cover alone, to refuse help — they compound. Sami isn't broken in a dramatic way. He's depleted. The night has emptied him out, and what's left is the elementary impulse: away. Not toward death, not toward freedom. Just away.]*

### Scene E3-2: The Tunnel Mouth

**Location:** A basement in one of the edge buildings — the tunnel entrance
**Time:** 4:45 AM
**Lighting:** Dark inside the building. Sami uses the flashlight — its beam cuts across a concrete floor, bare walls, a wooden door that leads down. Below the door: improvised steps, packed earth, timber bracing. The tunnel mouth.
**Ambient sound:** The building creaks. Earth settles. Below: a draught of air from the tunnel, cool, carrying the smell of soil and moisture and somewhere far away.

*The tunnel is real — one of the network that runs beneath the siege line. Some carry food, medicine, ammunition. Some carry people. They are narrow, dark, dangerous. Some have collapsed. Some are watched. But they exist, and they connect the inside to the outside, and right now the outside is everything the inside is not.*

*Sami descends the steps. The flashlight shows the tunnel entrance: a hole in the earth, roughly two metres wide, propped with timber beams, disappearing into darkness. The floor is packed dirt, worn smooth by passage. On the wall beside the entrance, someone has scratched words in the concrete:*

**[INTERACTION — TUNNEL WALL]**

> **Arabic:** كلمات محفورة بالحيط: "لا تنسونا." بخط حدا طالع من هون. ما بعرف مين كتبها ولا إمتى. بس هاد يلي كان بدّو يقولو: لا تنسونا.
> **English:** Words scratched into the wall: "Don't forget us." In the handwriting of someone leaving. I don't know who wrote them or when. But this is what they needed to say: don't forget us.

*Sami stands at the entrance. The flashlight beam reaches into the tunnel and finds nothing — the passage curves, and beyond the curve is black.*

### Scene E3-3: The Threshold

**Time:** 4:50 AM

*Behind him, up the steps, through the building, outside: the town. He can hear it — faintly, the sounds of morning. The azan has ended. The birds continue. Someone is alive out there, sweeping a threshold or carrying water or wondering where he is.*

*He thinks of Ahmad. Not a memory — a fact. Ahmad is in the town. Whatever is left of him — in the street where he fell, in the classroom where he taught, in the journal in Abu Yazan's keeping — it is here, in the siege, in the dark ring around Damascus. If Sami walks into this tunnel, he walks away from all of it.*

**[INTERIOR MONOLOGUE — TEXT ON SCREEN]**

> **Arabic:** في ناس بيطلعوا وبيحكوا وبيحملوا القصة معهن. وفي ناس بيضلّوا وبيكملوا. ما بعرف أيّا أنا. بس أعرف إنو إجريي واقفة هون — بين جوّا وبرّا — وما عم تقرر.
> **English:** Some people leave and they speak and they carry the story with them. And some people stay and they carry on. I don't know which I am. But I know my feet are standing here — between inside and outside — and they haven't decided.

*[ACTING NOTE: This is not a choice moment — the state variables have already decided. But it is a moment. Sami stands at the threshold. The player controls nothing. The game holds on this image: a man in a doorway of earth, the flashlight in his hand, the tunnel ahead, the town behind.]*

### Scene E3-4: The Descent

*Sami steps forward. Into the tunnel. The flashlight beam swings ahead of him, catching the timber props, the curved ceiling, the packed earth.*

**[GAMEPLAY — TUNNEL WALK]**

*The player walks Sami into the tunnel. The view is narrow — Sami fills most of the frame, the tunnel close around him. The flashlight begins to dim; the player must crank it. The mechanical sound of the crank — the familiar gameplay rhythm from the whole game — fills the tunnel.*

*Behind him, the tunnel entrance shrinks. A rectangle of grey light, the faintest hint of dawn. It gets smaller as Sami walks. Smaller.*

*The last sounds of the town — the birds, the morning — fade. What replaces them: earth, breath, the grind of the flashlight crank, footsteps on packed dirt.*

*The rectangle of light behind him is a slit now. A line. A point.*

*Gone.*

**[FINAL IMAGE — held for five seconds]**

*Total darkness. The sound of footsteps continuing. The sound of the flashlight being cranked — once, twice. A thin beam of light, showing only the tunnel ahead, curving, continuing, going somewhere that isn't here.*

**[FADE — the light fades as the flashlight winds down, natural, mechanical, until the screen is black]**

**[FINAL TEXT — white on black]**

> في ناس بتبقى وفي ناس بتمشي.
> مش كل مشي هروب.
> ومش كل بقاء شجاعة.

> *Some people stay and some people walk.*
> *Not every departure is flight.*
> *Not every staying is courage.*

**[HOLD — five seconds]**

**[FADE TO BLACK]**

## ENDING 4: الباقي / THE ONE WHO REMAINS

**Conditions:** `compassion >= 2 AND (um_ahmad_visited == true OR children_helped == true) AND (path != witness OR documented == false)`

### Scene E4-1: The Door

**Location:** The street outside Ahmad's basement classroom, eastern quarter
**Time:** 4:40 AM
**Lighting:** The dawn light reaches the street — golden, angled, the sun still low enough to paint the building fronts in warm colour while the street itself remains in shadow. The door to the basement classroom is set into the ground level of a three-storey building, half a flight of steps down from the pavement. The building is damaged — the upper floors have shell holes — but the basement is intact. It was always intact. The war is above; the classroom is below.
**Ambient sound:** Morning street sounds. Footsteps — other people's, not Sami's. A woman hanging washing on a line between two buildings, the fabric snapping. The sound of children — not close yet, but approaching.

*Sami doesn't remember deciding to come here. His night walk ended and he was here — at this door, these steps, this threshold. Ahmad's classroom.*

*[ACTING NOTE: The arrival should feel involuntary — the body knowing something the mind hasn't articulated. Sami's feet brought him here the way they brought him through the whole night: not with purpose but with gravity. This is where the weight of everything settles.]*

*He descends the half-flight of steps. The door is unlocked — Ahmad never locked it. Sami pushes it open.*

### Scene E4-2: The Classroom

**Location:** Ahmad's basement classroom
**Time:** 4:45 AM
**Lighting:** A single high window on the eastern wall lets in a rectangle of dawn light. The light falls on the floor — a warm, gold rectangle on grey concrete. The room is lit by this alone: natural light, the first in the game that isn't from a flashlight, a lighter, or a fire. The rest of the room is in soft shadow: visible, gentle, the darkness of a room rather than the darkness of a siege.
**Ambient sound:** Quiet. The street sounds are muffled by the half-underground walls. The room has its own acoustic: close, contained, a space that holds sound rather than letting it escape. A drip from a pipe somewhere — steady, metronomic, ordinary.

*The room is as Ahmad left it. This is important — no one has been here since yesterday morning when Ahmad locked the exercise books in the desk drawer, wrote tomorrow's lesson on the board, and walked out to check on the family in the southern quarter. The room is suspended in the moment of his leaving.*

*The player sees:*

- *A blackboard on the wall, chalked: a verb conjugation table. "كتب — يكتب — كاتب". To write — he writes — writer. Below it, in smaller writing, an exercise: "اكتب جملة عن يومك." Write a sentence about your day.*
- *Twelve chairs, small, child-sized, arranged in two arcs. Four of them are slightly askew — the last children to sit in them left them that way.*
- *A teacher's desk: a wooden table, scarred, with an exercise book on top, open to a marked page. A jar of chalk pieces. A pencil cup — three pencils, worn short. A glass of water, half-full, still.*
- *A stack of student exercise books against the wall, each labelled in a child's handwriting.*
- *A poster on the wall, hand-drawn: the Arabic alphabet, each letter illustrated. Alif with an apple, ba with a house (beit), ta with a fox (tha'lab).*

**[INTERACTION — THE CLASSROOM]**

> **Arabic:** صف أحمد. ما تغيّر شي من إمبارح الصبح. اللوح مكتوب عليه درس بكرا — درس ما صار. الكراسي ناطرة. الطبشورة ناطرة. كل شي ناطرو ييجي، وما رح ييجي.
> **English:** Ahmad's classroom. Nothing has changed since yesterday morning. The board has tomorrow's lesson on it — a lesson that never happened. The chairs are waiting. The chalk is waiting. Everything is waiting for him to come, and he's not coming.

**[INTERACTION — THE BLACKBOARD]**

> **Arabic:** "اكتب جملة عن يومك." هاد التمرين يلي كان بدّو يعطيهن ياه. شو كانوا رح يكتبوا ولاد الحصار عن يومهن؟ "اليوم أكلنا خبز من علف." "اليوم ما طلعنا عالشارع." "اليوم سمعنا قصف." جمل ما لازم يكتبها ولا ولد.
> **English:** "Write a sentence about your day." The exercise he was going to give them. What would siege children write about their day? "Today we ate bread made from feed." "Today we didn't go outside." "Today we heard shelling." Sentences no child should have to write.

**[INTERACTION — THE EXERCISE BOOKS]**

> **Arabic:** كراسيهن. كل واحد مكتوب عليه اسم بخط ولد. "حسن." "مريم." "عمر." "دعاء." أحمد كان يصحّح بالأحمر — بس تصحيحو لطيف. ما كان يحط غلط بكبير. كان يحط نجمة صغيرة حد الجمل الحلوة.
> **English:** Their exercise books. Each labelled in a child's hand. "Hassan." "Maryam." "Omar." "Du'aa." Ahmad corrected in red — but gently. He never wrote WRONG in big letters. He drew a small star next to the good sentences.

### Scene E4-3: The Boy

**Time:** 5:00 AM

*Sami has been standing in the classroom. How long, he doesn't know. The rectangle of light on the floor has moved — shifted slightly eastward as the sun climbs.*

*A sound: **footsteps on the stairs**. Small footsteps. The clap of plastic sandals on concrete. Then a figure in the doorway.*

*A **boy**, eight or nine years old. Thin, dark-eyed, wearing a t-shirt two sizes too large and shorts. He carries a school bag made from a repurposed rice sack, stitched along one side with red thread. He stands in the doorway and looks at Sami.*

*[ACTING NOTE: This is not a dramatic entrance. The boy is early — he's always early, the type who arrives first and sits in the front row. He came because this is Thursday and on Thursdays there is class. He doesn't know.]*

**الولد / The Boy:**
> *(looking past Sami at the empty room, then back at Sami)* أستاز أحمد وينو؟
> *(looking past Sami at the empty room, then back at Sami)* Where is teacher Ahmad?

*Silence. Sami looks at the boy. The boy looks at Sami. The question hangs in the room like the light hangs in the window — present, physical, unanswerable.*

*[ACTING NOTE: Sami doesn't answer the question. Not now. The boy will learn — today, tomorrow, from his mother, from another child, from the way the classroom is empty for too many days. But in this moment, in this room, the question stands. And Sami's answer is not words.]*

### Scene E4-4: The Chalk

*Sami turns to the blackboard. He looks at Ahmad's handwriting — the conjugation table, the exercise prompt. He looks at the chalk jar on the desk.*

*He picks up a piece of chalk. It's short, worn, the size of a thumb. It leaves white dust on his fingers.*

*He reaches up to the blackboard. In the upper-right corner, where Ahmad always wrote the date, Sami writes:*

> **١٥ آب ٢٠١٤**
> *15 August 2014*

*Today's date. The lesson that Ahmad would have written, if Ahmad were here. Sami is not a teacher. He doesn't know the curriculum, doesn't know what comes after the conjugation table, doesn't know what Ahmad planned. But he can write the date. He can open the room. He can be here when the children arrive.*

*The boy watches from the doorway. Then he walks in, sets his rice-sack bag on a chair — the front row, the same chair he always sits in — and sits down. He folds his hands on the desk. He is ready for class.*

**[GAMEPLAY — THE CHALK]**

*The player controls Sami writing the date on the board. A slow, deliberate action — the chalk moving across the slate, the numbers forming. The mechanical scratch of chalk on board fills the room.*

*[No further gameplay. No further choices. The game holds.]*

### Scene E4-5: The Morning

**Time:** 5:10 AM

*More footsteps on the stairs. Two more children appear: a girl carrying a younger brother on her hip, the brother chewing on a piece of bread. They see Sami at the board. They hesitate. The boy already seated turns:*

**الولد:**
> *(to the new arrivals, matter-of-fact)* تعوا. فوت.
> *(to the new arrivals, matter-of-fact)* Come in. Sit down.

*They come in. They sit.*

*Sami stands at the board, chalk in hand. Ahmad's journal — if he has it — is open on the desk, open to a lesson plan page. If he doesn't have the journal, the exercise books are there, and the conjugation table, and the work of figuring out what comes next.*

*The rectangle of light from the window reaches the desk. It touches the chalk jar, the pencil cup, the glass of water. The room is warm with morning.*

**[FINAL IMAGE — slow zoom out, held for eight seconds]**

*Sami at the blackboard, the chalk in his hand, turning from the board toward the children. Three children in their chairs, a fourth arriving at the door. The exercise books on the desk. Through the small basement window, the morning light falls in a gold rectangle on the floor. On the board: the date, and below it, in Sami's handwriting — not Ahmad's, a different hand, less practised — the beginning of a lesson.*

**[FADE — slow, over ten seconds]**

**[FINAL TEXT — white on black]**

> الحياة مش بس إنك تضل عايش.
> الحياة إنك تضل إنسان.

> *Life isn't just staying alive.*
> *Life is staying human.*

**[HOLD — five seconds]**

**[FADE TO BLACK]**

## ENDING 5: ساعة الذئب / THE WOLF'S HOUR

**Conditions:** `(negotiation_outcome == failure) OR (isolation >= 2 AND compassion <= 1 AND courage >= 2) OR (fallback — no other ending conditions met)`

### Scene E5-1: The Rubble

**Location:** A partially collapsed building — residential, three storeys, the middle floor pancaked. Sami is on the ground floor, in what was a living room.
**Time:** 4:30 AM
**Lighting:** The space is enclosed — walls on three sides, the fourth open where the façade collapsed outward, leaving the room exposed to the sky like a stage. Through the opening: the pre-dawn sky, lightening from the east. Inside the room: shadow, grey concrete, rebar hanging like roots from the ceiling. The rubble is settled — this building fell months ago. Dust has become dirt. A vine has started to grow through the cracks.
**Ambient sound:** Silence — the deep, humming silence of a ruined building where the structure has finished moving. Occasional settling: a pebble falling, concrete dust sifting. Wind through the opening. The distant fajr azan, reaching this place as an echo of an echo.

*Sami sits on a slab of concrete, his back against a standing wall. He's been here for some time — since the night walk brought him here, or since the failed negotiation left him with nothing, or since the rooftop, when he climbed down and just... stopped.*

*[ACTING NOTE: This is the quiet that isn't peace. It's the quiet of depletion — everything spent, everything emptied out. Sami isn't deciding not to move. He has simply stopped. The night took everything and this is what remains: a body in rubble, breathing, looking at a sky that is changing whether he watches or not.]*

*His tools are around him — the flashlight, its crank still, the battery exhausted. The mirror shard in his pocket. The walkie-talkie, which has been silent for hours. The lighter, if he has it. Objects that were useful and are now just objects.*

### Scene E5-2: The Cat

**Time:** 4:40 AM

*Movement at the edge of the opening. A shape on the rubble — low, fluid, picking its way over broken concrete with the care of something that has learned every surface in this terrain.*

*The **cat**. The thin tabby with the torn ear. It appears at the room's open edge, pauses, regards Sami. The same cat from Act 1, from the night walk. Two meetings before; this is the third.*

*It picks its way across the rubble toward Sami — not rushing, not cautious. The movement of an animal that has assessed the situation and found it acceptable. It reaches the slab where Sami sits. It sniffs the air near his hand. Then it settles — not touching him, not in his lap, but near. Close enough to feel. It sits upright, facing the opening, the way cats sit when they have agreed to share a space but not to acknowledge it.*

*[ACTING NOTE: The cat is not a redemption. It is not a message. It is a cat, doing what cats do: finding a warm body in a cold morning and sitting near it. The meaning, if there is meaning, is the player's to assign. What matters is the fact of company — wordless, unasked-for, animal. Two creatures in rubble, surviving the same hour.]*

**[GAMEPLAY — NO INTERACTION, NO TIMER]**

*The player cannot interact. There is no prompt, no button, no objective. Sami sits. The cat sits. The sky changes. The game waits.*

*After a minimum of twenty seconds of real time:*

*The cat **yawns**. A full, extravagant, teeth-baring yawn — the yawn of a creature that is not in danger, not in distress, not concerned with anything beyond the present moment. The yawn ends. The cat settles again, tucking its paws beneath its body. Its eyes close halfway — not sleeping, but allowing sleep to be possible.*

### Scene E5-3: The Azan

**Time:** 4:50 AM

*The fajr azan sounds from somewhere — closer than the earlier echo. A man's voice, unaccompanied, rising and falling. It reaches the ruined building and fills the space the way sound fills a broken vessel: unevenly, with gaps and resonances.*

**Sound:** The azan. "الله أكبر... الله أكبر... أشهد أن لا إله إلا الله..." The voice is not beautiful in the way trained voices are beautiful. It is beautiful in the way a human sound is beautiful at 4:50 in the morning in a town that has been bombed for two years and is still, despite everything, making the call.

*The cat's ears swivel toward the sound. It does not move.*

*Sami listens. He doesn't pray — the game doesn't show whether he does or doesn't. He listens. The call is ancient and it is happening now and it is the sound of someone saying: we are still here. God or no God, belief or no belief — the call is an act of presence. Someone climbed a minaret or a rooftop, in the dark, in a siege, and opened their mouth and said: we are still here.*

### Scene E5-4: The Light

**Time:** 5:00 AM
**Lighting:** The sun clears the horizon. The light enters the ruined room through the collapsed façade — a diagonal beam, catching dust motes, illuminating the standing wall behind Sami. The light is warm, golden, the specific warmth of August morning in the Levant. It reaches the rubble, the vine growing through the crack, the cat's fur (orange, in this light, instead of grey), and Sami's hands, which rest on his knees.

*The light does what light does: it makes things visible. It doesn't heal them. The rubble is still rubble. The building is still destroyed. The siege is still here. But the darkness is gone, and in its absence, the shapes of things are gentler. The hard edges of the night soften into the ordinary geometry of a damaged town at morning.*

*Sounds — more of them now. The town's waking. A child's voice, far away, calling someone. A door banging. The clatter of a pot — someone cooking. A woman's voice singing, very faintly, just a phrase of a melody, then stopping. Life, insisting.*

*The cat opens its eyes fully. It stretches — front paws out, back arched, a full luxury of a stretch. Then it stands, gives Sami a last look — neither meaningful nor meaningless — and walks across the rubble to the opening. It jumps down to the street and is gone.*

*Sami watches it go.*

### Scene E5-5: The Stillness

**Time:** 5:05 AM

*Sami doesn't move. He will — eventually. The day will make its demands: water needs carrying, pipes need patching, someone needs something, and the body responds to need even when the self has gone quiet. But not yet.*

*For now he sits in the rubble, in the light, in the morning, in the siege. His hands on his knees. His eyes open, looking at the sky through the opening where the wall used to be — the sky that is blue now, the pale clean blue of a day that hasn't happened yet.*

*The azan fades. The birds continue. Somewhere in the town, a child laughs — a sound so ordinary, so unrelated to anything, that it almost doesn't register. Almost.*

**[FINAL IMAGE — held for ten seconds]**

*Sami sitting in the rubble, alone now — the cat has gone. The dawn light fills the room through the opening. The town is waking around him: sounds of life, fragile but persistent, coming from every direction. The vine grows through the crack. Sami's eyes are open, looking at nothing in particular, looking at everything. His face is neither broken nor whole. It is the face of a person who has survived a night and does not yet know what that means.*

*The sounds of the town continue — layers of them, building: footsteps, doors, voices, a child, a rooster, a generator, the clang of a pipe being struck, water running. The ordinary, impossible, daily orchestra of a place that refuses to stop.*

**[FADE — very slow, over fifteen seconds. The image remains as the sound fades first, then the light, then the shape of the man in the rubble, until only the sky remains, blue, empty, continuing.]**

**[FINAL TEXT — white on black]**

> ما خلصت القصة.
> بس خلص الليل.

> *The story didn't end.*
> *But the night did.*

**[HOLD — five seconds]**

**[FADE TO BLACK]**

## Act 4 — Technical Summary

### Duration
- Part 1 (Descent): 2–3 minutes (all paths)
- Part 2 (Final Choice): 1 minute (conditional — some players skip)
- Ending sequence: 6–10 minutes (varies by ending)
- **Total: 10–15 minutes**

### Ending Distribution by Path

| Prior Path | Likely Ending | Alternate |
|---|---|---|
| Witness + Camera (C2/E1) | **Ending 1: The Witness** | — |
| Retrieval + Negotiation success (C1/D1/G1 or G3) | **Ending 2: The Last Farewell** | Ending 5 if negotiation failed |
| Retrieval + Dangerous route (C1/D2) | **Ending 2** (variant — body retrieved without negotiation) | Ending 5 if route failed or heavy cost |
| Grief + Isolation (C3/F2) | **Ending 3: The Tunnel** | Ending 5 if courage ≥ 2 |
| High compassion + Um Ahmad (any path/F1 or B1+A1) | **Ending 4: The One Who Remains** | — |
| Anger negotiation failure (G2) | **Ending 5: The Wolf's Hour** | — |
| Mixed/unclear state | **Choice H offered** → resolves to ending | Ending 5 as fallback |

### State Variable Resolution

```
ENDING SELECTION (final):

if documented AND path == witness:
    → Ending 1 (The Witness)

elif body_retrieved AND (negotiation_outcome == success OR dangerous_route_complete):
    → Ending 2 (The Last Farewell)

elif isolation >= 2 AND compassion <= 1:
    if courage <= 1:
        → Ending 3 (The Tunnel)
    else:
        → Ending 5 (The Wolf's Hour)

elif compassion >= 2 AND (um_ahmad_visited OR children_helped):
    → Ending 4 (The One Who Remains)

else:
    → Ending 5 (The Wolf's Hour)  # default/fallback
```

### Tools Used
| Tool | Scene |
|---|---|
| Hand-crank flashlight | Descent from rooftop; Ending 3 (tunnel) |
| Camera | Ending 1 (photo review, carried out) |
| White cloth | Ending 2 (the retrieval crossing) |
| Lighter | Present in inventory; no required use |
| Ahmad's journal | Ending 2 (the poem at the burial); Ending 4 (open on desk) |

### Key Emotional Beats
1. **The Two Boys** — children heading to a class that will never happen, passing Sami without knowing
2. **The Retrieval Crossing** — ninety seconds of silence, carrying a friend across a kill zone
3. **Maher's Gesture** — turning away (or not) as the body passes
4. **Um Ahmad's Hand** — pressing flat on the earth, holding or feeling
5. **The Darwish Poem** — "On this earth, there is that which deserves life"
6. **The Tunnel Wall** — "Don't forget us"
7. **The Boy's Question** — "Where is teacher Ahmad?"
8. **The Date on the Board** — ١٥ آب ٢٠١٤ — the act of continuity
9. **The Cat's Yawn** — peace without meaning
10. **The Epilogue** — "The characters are fictional. The siege was real."

### Music / Score Notes
- **The Descent:** No score. Natural ambient — birds, wind, the azan.
- **Ending 1 (Witness):** A single oud string, plucked once at the moment Sami sees the photo of Ahmad's shoes. Nothing else until he begins walking. Then a very quiet, sustained ney line — the same breath-phrase from the Act 3 night walk — accompanying him east. It fades as the figure grows small.
- **Ending 2 (Farewell):** Silence during the retrieval crossing. During the burial: Abu Yazan's recitation, unaccompanied. When Sami reads the poem: nothing. After the poem, as the camera pulls back to the grape vine: a single note — a qanun string, high, clear, ringing — that sustains and slowly decays. It does not resolve.
- **Ending 3 (Tunnel):** The mechanical sound of the flashlight crank is the only rhythm. As the light from the entrance shrinks, the crank sounds become the entire sound world — percussive, human, diminishing. When the light is gone: silence, then the crank once more, then nothing.
- **Ending 4 (Remains):** Silence in the classroom. The scratch of chalk on board. After the children enter: the sound of chairs on concrete, the rustle of exercise books, a child's cough. These are the score. The music is the sound of a room resuming its purpose.
- **Ending 5 (Wolf's Hour):** Wind and settling rubble. The cat's movements are the only non-ambient sound — paw on concrete, the breath of the yawn. The azan enters and fills the space. As the town wakes: a slow accumulation of human sounds — each one specific, each one the sound of life continuing. This accumulation is the score. It builds not to a crescendo but to a texture: the ordinary, ongoing, impossible sound of people surviving.

### Emotional Register
- **Ending 1:** Purposeful. Forward motion. The weight of responsibility, carried willingly.
- **Ending 2:** Grief honoured. The body brought home. The small ceremony in the grape arbour. Loss given its shape.
- **Ending 3:** Departure. Not defeat, not flight — a different kind of survival. The ambiguity of leaving.
- **Ending 4:** Continuity. The act of stepping into the space the dead leave behind. Not replacing — continuing.
- **Ending 5:** Stillness. The aftermath without resolution. The night ended, the day will come, and for now there is only this: a body, a breath, a light.

### Design Note on Multiple Endings

None of the five endings is presented as the "correct" or "best" ending. The game does not rank them, score them, or indicate preference. Each is a truthful response to the circumstances the player created through their choices. The Witness is not better than the Wolf's Hour; the Tunnel is not worse than the Farewell. They are different survivals, different ways of carrying the weight of a night that should not have been necessary.

The player may replay and reach different endings. The game does not reward this — no achievement, no completion percentage, no "all endings seen" badge. Each playthrough is complete in itself. The story is about one night, and one night is enough.
