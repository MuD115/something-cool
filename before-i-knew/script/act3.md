# Act 3: The Night — الليل

**Full production script — 20–25 minutes of play**
**Time:** 8:00 PM – 3:00 AM, 14–15 August 2014
**Setting:** The besieged town — the frontline, the streets, the rooftops, the spaces between waking and sleep

*Act 3 is the longest act. It contains the game's moral centrepiece — the negotiation with Officer Maher — alongside shared sequences (the night walk, the visions, the flashbacks, the rooftop) that all paths experience in different arrangements. The act moves from dusk into deep night and ends before dawn.*

---

## TITLE CARD

> الليل وصل.
> *Night has arrived.*

> بالحصار، الليل مش بس عتمة. الليل هوي المكان يلي بتطلع فيه الأشياء يلي الشمس بتخبّيها.
> *Under siege, night isn't just darkness. Night is the place where the things the sun hides come out.*

## PART 1: THE NEGOTIATION — المفاوضة

*[RETRIEVAL PATH, D1 ONLY — Sami has the white cloth]*

### Scene 3A-1: The Approach

**Location:** The no-man's-land edge — the last wall before open ground
**Time:** 8:15 PM
**Lighting:** Post-sunset. The sky is deep indigo to the west, darkening to black overhead. No streetlights — no electricity for months. The only illumination: the moon, three-quarters full, low on the eastern horizon, casting long pale shadows. The regime checkpoint ahead has a single light — a battery-powered lamp on a sandbag wall, creating a small yellow pool.
**Ambient sound:** Dramatically quieter than daytime. The shelling has stopped. Occasional gunfire — single shots, very distant. Crickets, extraordinarily loud in the silence. The creak of damaged buildings settling in the cooling air. Sami's breathing.

*[ACTING NOTE: Night changes everything. The town that Sami has navigated all day becomes alien. Shapes are uncertain. Doorways are black voids. The moon throws shadows that move as clouds cross it. The silence is not peaceful — it is the silence of a place where sound draws fire.]*

### Gameplay

**[TOOL: HAND-CRANK FLASHLIGHT — FIRST USE]**

*Before stepping into the open, Sami needs to see the ground ahead. The hand-crank flashlight is introduced.*

> **[Crank flashlight]** — لف الكشّاف / Crank the flashlight

*The player holds the crank button. The light builds — a pale yellow-white beam. When the player releases, it begins to fade. The light must be periodically recranked.*

*[TUTORIAL: The hand-crank flashlight mechanic. The light fades over about eight seconds of real time. The player must balance using the light with the knowledge that light draws attention.]*

*Sami checks the ground: rubble, glass, a crater's edge. He switches the flashlight off. He picks up the white cloth on its pole.*

**[GAMEPLAY — THE CROSSING]**

*Sami steps into the open.*

*The player walks forward, slowly. Sami holds the white cloth above his head with one hand. The cloth catches the moonlight — pale, visible. He walks toward the checkpoint light.*

*The distance is sixty metres. The player walks it in real time — about twenty seconds. No crouching. No running. Upright, exposed, holding a bedsheet on a stick. Every step is a bet.*

*[ACTING NOTE: This should feel like the longest walk of the player's life. No cover. The sniper is somewhere above. The regime soldiers ahead can see him coming. The white cloth is tradition, not guarantee. The player's instinct will be to crouch, to run, to take cover — the game doesn't let them. They walk.]*

**Sound:** Sami's footsteps on gravel. His breathing, audible, controlled. The crickets. The cloth snapping in a light wind. Nothing else.

At thirty metres, a **shout** from the checkpoint:

**صوت جندي / Soldier's voice:**
> *(sharp, Arabic with a coastal accent)* مين هاد؟! وقّف!
> *(sharp, Arabic with a coastal accent)* Who's that?! Stop!

**سامي:**
> *(loudly, clearly)* مدني! مدني! عندي قماشة بيضا! بدي احكي مع الضابط!
> *(loudly, clearly)* Civilian! Civilian! I have a white cloth! I want to speak with the officer!

A beat of silence. Voices conferring behind the sandbags — indistinct, urgent.

**صوت جندي:**
> ارفع إيديك! ضل واقف!
> *Raise your hands! Stay where you are!*

*Sami raises both hands, the cloth pole in one. He stands in the moonlit open, arms up, waiting.*

**Sound:** A long, held silence. The wind. The cloth.

Two **soldiers** emerge from behind the sandbags. They approach — cautious, rifles pointed but not aimed. They're young, early twenties. Dust-covered uniforms, tired faces. One of them pats Sami down, takes the walkie-talkie and the lighter. They leave the cloth.

**جندي ١ / Soldier 1:**
> *(to his colleague)* خبّر الضابط.
> *(to his colleague)* Tell the officer.

*They bring Sami to the checkpoint. Behind the sandbags: a small area — a chair, a table, a thermos, a radio. Military clutter. A flag on a pole. The battery lamp throws harsh shadows.*

### Scene 3A-2: Officer Maher

**Location:** The regime checkpoint — behind the sandbags
**Time:** 8:25 PM
**Lighting:** The single battery lamp. Everything outside its pool is darkness. Maher's face is half-lit, half-shadow. The moon is behind the checkpoint, casting a pale glow over the town behind Sami.
**Ambient sound:** The radio crackling with military communications — call signs, coordinates, clipped voices. The soldiers shifting. Maher's chair creaking as he leans back.

**الضابط ماهر / Officer Maher** sits on a folding chair behind a camp table. He is exactly as described: thirty-five, lean, tired. His uniform is worn but maintained — a man who still irons his creases because it's the last structure he has. He looks at Sami the way you look at a problem that has walked into your evening.

**ماهر:**
> *(studying Sami, not hostile, not warm)* شو بدك؟
> *(studying Sami, not hostile, not warm)* What do you want?

**سامي:**
> في جثة بالشارع. صاحبي. بدي جيبو.
> *There's a body in the street. My friend. I want to bring him back.*

**ماهر:**
> صاحبك؟ مين كان صاحبك؟
> *Your friend? Who was your friend?*

**سامي:**
> اسمو خالد. كان معلّم.
> *His name was Khaled. He was a teacher.*

**ماهر:**
> *(a short, humourless exhale)* كلهن معلمين. أو أطباء. أو صحفيين. ما حدا بيقول إنو كان مسلّح.
> *(a short, humourless exhale)* They're all teachers. Or doctors. Or journalists. No one ever says he was armed.

**سامي:**
> لأنو ما كان مسلّح. كان يعلّم ولاد عربي بقبو مدرسة.
> *Because he wasn't armed. He was teaching children Arabic in a school basement.*

**ماهر:**
> بقبو مدرسة. وين؟
> *In a school basement. Where?*

**سامي:**
> بالحارة الشرقية.
> *In the eastern quarter.*

**ماهر:**
> *(making a note on a scrap of paper — or pretending to)* والمسلحين يلي بالحارة الشرقية — مين بيعلّمهن؟
> *(making a note on a scrap of paper — or pretending to)* And the militants in the eastern quarter — who teaches them?

*[ACTING NOTE: Maher's cynicism is practised but not total. He's heard this claim a hundred times — "he was a civilian, he was a teacher." Some of those times it was true. Some of those times it wasn't. He's lost the ability to tell, and that loss is part of what the war has cost him.]*

**سامي:**
> *(controlled)* صاحبي كان يعلّم ولاد عمرهن تمان سنين. اسماءهن: رامي، سارة، يوسف، ليلى، عمر. بدك كمّل؟
> *(controlled)* My friend taught children aged eight. Their names: Rami, Sara, Youssef, Layla, Omar. Shall I go on?

*A pause. Something shifts in Maher's face — not much, but enough.*

**ماهر:**
> *(leaning back)* قعود.
> *(leaning back)* Sit down.

*There's a second folding chair, the kind you'd find at a school event. Sami sits. They face each other across the camp table. The lamp between them.*

*[ACTING NOTE: This is the scene. Two men at a table. A lamp. The darkness around them. Everything that follows happens in this small circle of light.]*

---

### Scene 3A-3: The Conversation

**Location:** Same — the checkpoint table
**Time:** 8:30 PM – 9:15 PM

*This is a dialogue scene with branching options. Maher and Sami talk. The player chooses Sami's approach at key junctures, leading to Choice G (the negotiation approach). The conversation flows through several beats regardless of choice, but the tone and outcome depend on how the player handles it.*

### Beat 1: The Terms

**ماهر:**
> *(pouring tea from the thermos — offering a glass to Sami, a gesture of custom, not kindness)* بدك تجيب الجثة.
> *(pouring tea from the thermos — offering a glass to Sami, a gesture of custom, not kindness)* You want to retrieve the body.

**سامي:**
> إي.
> *Yes.*

**ماهر:**
> ليش؟
> *Why?*

**سامي:**
> لأنو لازم يندفن. أمو بدها تدفنو.
> *Because he needs to be buried. His mother wants to bury him.*

**ماهر:**
> *(sipping tea)* أمو. هي كمان بالحصار؟
> *(sipping tea)* His mother. She's in the siege too?

**سامي:**
> إي.
> *Yes.*

**ماهر:**
> ليش ما طلعت؟
> *Why didn't she leave?*

**سامي:**
> *(steadily)* لأنو هاد بيتها.
> *(steadily)* Because this is her home.

*A beat. Maher drinks his tea.*

**ماهر:**
> بتعرف شو بيصير لو سمحتلك تاخد الجثة؟ رح يقولوا عني إنّي عم بساعد إرهابيين. رح يكون في تحقيق. يمكن يبعتوني ع خط تاني — واحد ما في رجعة منو.
> *You know what happens if I let you take the body? They'll say I'm helping terrorists. There'll be an investigation. They might send me to another front — one you don't come back from.*

*[ACTING NOTE: Maher is being frank. He's not performing cruelty. He's calculating — risk to himself, risk to his position, risk to his family. Every decision at this checkpoint is a survival equation.]*

### Beat 2: The Teacher and the Soldier

**سامي:**
> خالد ما كان إرهابي. كان يعلّم ولاد قواعد عربي.
> *Khaled wasn't a terrorist. He taught children Arabic grammar.*

**ماهر:**
> *(flatly)* والمظاهرات؟
> *(flatly)* And the protests?*

**سامي:**
> المظاهرات كانت سلمية.
> *The protests were peaceful.*

**ماهر:**
> *(a bitter laugh)* سلمية. وبعدها؟
> *(a bitter laugh)* Peaceful. And after?*

*A pause. The truth is complicated. Sami knows it. Maher knows he knows it.*

**سامي:**
> خالد ما حمل سلاح بحياتو.
> *Khaled never carried a weapon in his life.*

**ماهر:**
> *(studying Sami)* وإنت؟
> *(studying Sami)* And you?

**سامي:**
> أنا مهندس. بصلّح مواسير مي.
> *I'm an engineer. I fix water pipes.*

**ماهر:**
> مهندس. مواسير مي.
> *An engineer. Water pipes.*

*He says it as though tasting the words for plausibility.*

**ماهر:**
> شو كنت تدرس قبل ما يصير هالشي كلو؟
> *What were you studying before all this happened?*

**سامي:**
> هندسة مدنية. بجامعة دمشق.
> *Civil engineering. At Damascus University.*

**ماهر:**
> *(a flicker of something — recognition, maybe)* جامعة دمشق. أنا كنت طالب بالكلية العسكرية لمّا كان عندكن الاعتصامات. شو كان اسمو — المنشد يلي كان يغني عالدرج؟
> *(a flicker of something — recognition, maybe)* Damascus University. I was at the military academy when you had those sit-ins. What was his name — the singer who used to sing on the steps?

*Sami doesn't answer. This is dangerous territory — acknowledging protests is an admission.*

**ماهر:**
> *(waving his hand)* ما بهمّني. كان زمان.
> *(waving his hand)* Doesn't matter. That was a long time ago.

### Beat 3: The Personal

*Maher shifts in his chair. He reaches into his chest pocket and takes out a photograph — creased, soft from handling. He looks at it, then sets it on the table. Two young girls, the older maybe six, the younger four. They're in a garden. The older one is making a face at the camera. The younger one is mid-laugh.*

**ماهر:**
> بناتي. ريم ورنا. ما شفتهن من خمس أشهر.
> *My daughters. Reem and Rana. I haven't seen them in five months.*

*He doesn't offer the photo for Sami to hold. He sets it where both of them can see it.*

**ماهر:**
> بتعرف ليش أنا هون؟ مش لأني بحب النظام. مش لأني بكره الناس يلي هونيك. *(gestures toward Ghouta)* أنا هون لأنو إذا ما كنت هون — شو بيصير لهنّ؟ *(taps the photo)*
> *You know why I'm here? Not because I love the regime. Not because I hate the people over there. *(gestures toward Ghouta)* I'm here because if I'm not here — what happens to them? *(taps the photo)*

*[ACTING NOTE: This is real. Maher is not manipulating. He is a man from a poor Alawite village who joined the army because it was the only route out. His family is marked by his service. If the regime falls, the retribution will find them. He is complicit in a siege that starves children because he believes — perhaps correctly — that the alternative is his own children's deaths. The player should feel the weight of this without being asked to agree with it.]*

**ماهر:**
> أنا ما اخترت هالحرب. بس إذا ما كملت فيها — بناتي بيدفعوا التمن.
> *I didn't choose this war. But if I don't keep going — my daughters pay the price.*

### CHOICE G — The Negotiation Approach

*A long beat. Sami holds Maher's gaze. The lamp flickers. Three options:*

> **G1:** بكرامة / With dignity — *[Sami speaks calmly, holds the weight without breaking it]*

> **G2:** بغضب / With anger — *[Sami cannot contain it — the injustice spills out]*

> **G3:** بصفقة / With a deal — *[Sami offers something practical — a concession]*

---

#### Branch G1: Dignity — بكرامة

**سامي:**
> *(calmly, looking at the photo)* حلوين بناتك.
> *(calmly, looking at the photo)* Your daughters are beautiful.

*Maher's face tightens, briefly — vulnerability exposed.*

**سامي:**
> وأنا بفهم. إنت خايف عليهن. وعم تساوي يلي بتقدر عليه تحميهن.
> *And I understand. You're afraid for them. And you're doing what you can to protect them.*

*A pause.*

**سامي:**
> بس ريم ورنا — كم عمرهن؟ ست وأربعة؟ في ولاد نفس عمرهن هونيك. *(gestures back toward Ghouta)* عم ياكلوا علف بقر. عم ينامو بأقبية. عم يجمّعوا حديد تيشتروا أكل. أنا شفتهن اليوم. ما بدي إياك تحس بالذنب — بدي إياك تعرف.
> *But Reem and Rana — how old are they? Six and four? There are children their age over there. *(gestures back toward Ghouta)* They're eating animal feed. They're sleeping in basements. They're collecting scrap metal to buy food. I saw them today. I don't want you to feel guilty — I want you to know.*

*A long silence. Maher looks at the photo. Then at Sami. Then at the darkness beyond the checkpoint.*

**ماهر:**
> *(very quietly)* بعرف.
> *(very quietly)* I know.

*The radio crackles. Maher ignores it.*

**ماهر:**
> *(after another silence)* خود صاحبك. قبل الفجر. بالعتمة. ما بشوف شي.
> *(after another silence)* Take your friend. Before dawn. In the dark. I won't see anything.

*He picks up the photo and puts it back in his pocket.*

**ماهر:**
> *(not looking at Sami)* ولا ترجع.
> *(not looking at Sami)* And don't come back.

*[ACTING NOTE: "I won't see anything" — Maher is giving permission without giving permission. He is maintaining plausible deniability while allowing a man to bury his friend. This is the furthest he can go. It costs him something, and Sami should see that cost without naming it.]*

> **[STATE: negotiation_outcome = success, officer_connection = true, body_retrieved = true]**

**سامي:**
> *(standing)* شكراً.
> *(standing)* Thank you.

**ماهر:**
> *(sharp)* ما بدي شكرك. روح.
> *(sharp)* I don't want your thanks. Go.

*The soldiers return Sami's lighter and walkie-talkie. They watch him walk back into the dark, the white cloth folded under his arm. The checkpoint light shrinks behind him.*

---

#### Branch G2: Anger — بغضب

**سامي:**
> *(voice rising)* بناتك — عم تحكيلي عن بناتك؟ وبنات الناس يلي عم تحاصرهن شو؟ ما عندهن بنات؟
> *(voice rising)* Your daughters — you're telling me about your daughters? And the daughters of the people you're besieging — what about them? Don't they have daughters?

*Maher's face hardens.*

**سامي:**
> عم تجوّعوا ولاد! عم ترموا براميل ع بيوت! وإنت قاعد هون عم تشرب شاي وتقلّي عن بناتك؟
> *You're starving children! You're dropping barrels on houses! And you sit here drinking tea and telling me about your daughters?*

**ماهر:**
> *(standing, chair scraping)* اخترت كلامك.
> *(standing, chair scraping)* Choose your words.

**سامي:**
> *(not stopping)* كل يوم عم يموتوا ناس. كل يوم. والعالم عم يتفرّج. وإنتو — إنتو عم تساعدوا.
> *(not stopping)* Every day people are dying. Every day. And the world watches. And you — you're helping it happen.

*The soldiers step closer. One puts a hand on his rifle. Maher raises a hand — a signal to wait.*

**ماهر:**
> *(very controlled, but his voice has an edge)* بتعرف شو صار لمّا آخر واحد جاءني بهالحكي؟ رجّعوه بصندوق. إنت عندك فرصة وحدة — إنك تمشي من هون بحياتك.
> *(very controlled, but his voice has an edge)* You know what happened to the last man who came to me with this talk? They sent him back in a box. You have one chance — to walk out of here alive.

*A silence. Then — as Maher leans forward — the photograph slips from his chest pocket. It falls to the table. The two girls' faces, lit by the lamp.*

*Both men look at it. The anger in the air shifts — doesn't disappear, but cracks. The photograph is so small, so ordinary. Two children in a garden.*

*Sami breathes. Maher picks up the photo. His hand trembles, slightly — barely visible.*

**ماهر:**
> *(sitting back down, slowly)* روح.
> *(sitting back down, slowly)* Go.

**سامي:**
> وصاحبي؟
> *And my friend?*

**ماهر:**
> *(a long pause, looking at the photo)* ...بالفجر. ما بضمنلك شي. بس إذا ما شافك حدا...
> *(a long pause, looking at the photo)* ...At dawn. I can't guarantee anything. But if no one sees you...

*He trails off. It's not permission. It's the closest thing to permission he can offer after what Sami said.*

> **[STATE: negotiation_outcome = partial, officer_connection = false]**

*The soldiers return Sami's things. He walks into the dark. Behind him, Maher is still holding the photo.*

---

#### Branch G3: Pragmatic Deal — بصفقة

**سامي:**
> *(leaning forward, lowering his voice)* بقدر ساعدك بشي.
> *(leaning forward, lowering his voice)* I can help you with something.

*Maher's eyes narrow. Interest.*

**ماهر:**
> شو عندك؟
> *What do you have?*

**سامي:**
> أنا بعرف شبكة المي تبعت هالمنطقة. كل أنبوب، كل محبس، كل خط. إذا بدك تقطع المي ع حي معيّن — أو توصّلها — أنا بقدر قلّك من وين.
> *I know the water network of this area. Every pipe, every valve, every line. If you want to cut the water to a specific block — or reconnect it — I can tell you where.*

*[ACTING NOTE: This is the moral compromise. Sami is offering the regime control over the water infrastructure — his expertise, the thing he has maintained to keep people alive. He is trading it for one body. The weight of this should be unbearable.]*

*Maher considers. He taps his fingers on the table.*

**ماهر:**
> الشبكة المحلية. المحابس الرئيسية.
> *The local network. The main valves.*

**سامي:**
> *(hating himself)* إي.
> *(hating himself)* Yes.

**ماهر:**
> *(a beat, then)* أعطيني خريطة. ما بدها تكون كاملة — بس المحابس الرئيسية يلي بتتحكّم بالحارة الجنوبية.
> *(a beat, then)* Give me a map. It doesn't have to be complete — just the main valves that control the southern quarter.

*Sami draws on a scrap of paper. His hand is steady. His stomach is not.*

**ماهر:**
> *(taking the paper, folding it)* خود صاحبك. الليلة. هلّق.
> *(taking the paper, folding it)* Take your friend. Tonight. Now.

*[ACTING NOTE: Maher doesn't hesitate once the deal is concrete. This is a transaction. He understands transactions. It's cleaner than morality.]*

> **[STATE: negotiation_outcome = success, body_retrieved = true, officer_connection = false]**

*Sami walks into the dark. He has what he came for. He has also given something that may cost other people water. He carries this knowledge in his body like a stone.*

## PART 2: THE DANGEROUS ROUTE — الطريق الخطر

*[RETRIEVAL PATH, D2 ONLY — Sami takes the back route through the bombed building]*

### Scene 3B-1: The Building

**Location:** The six-storey building overlooking the no-man's-land
**Time:** 8:30 PM
**Lighting:** Near-total darkness inside the building. Moonlight enters through holes in the walls and roof, creating isolated pools of pale light. Dust hangs in the beams. The flashlight is essential.
**Ambient sound:** The building groans — structural sounds, metal under stress, concrete settling. Dripping water from a broken pipe somewhere. The wind through open floors creates a low, shifting moan. Distant cricket song from outside.

### Gameplay

**[GAMEPLAY — TRAVERSAL SEQUENCE: THE BUILDING]**

This is the game's most demanding physical sequence. Sami must navigate upward through a severely damaged building to reach a vantage point over the street where Khaled lies.

**[TOOL: FLASHLIGHT — ESSENTIAL]**

*The player must crank the flashlight repeatedly. The light reveals: collapsed stairwells, tilted floors, exposed rebar, gaps in the floor that drop two storeys. Without light, the player cannot see the obstacles.*

**Floor 1 → Floor 2:**
- The main stairwell is intact. Climb normally.
- At the top: a hallway with one apartment door hanging open. Inside: an abandoned home — a table set for dinner, plates still on it, covered in dust and plaster. A child's drawing pinned to a wall. The family left — or didn't.

**[INTERACTION — ABANDONED TABLE]**

> **Arabic:** طاولة محطوط عليها صحون. كانوا عم يتعشّوا — أو رايحين يتعشّوا. ما رجعوا.
> **English:** A table with plates set on it. They were eating dinner — or about to. They didn't come back.

**Floor 2 → Floor 3:**
- The stairwell is blocked — a concrete slab has fallen across it.
- **[Climb]** — The player must climb over the slab, using rebar as handholds. The slab shifts slightly under weight.
- **Sound:** A deep grinding. Dust falls. The slab settles. It holds.

**Floor 3:**
- The third floor has partially collapsed. The floor tilts at fifteen degrees. Furniture has slid to one wall. A wardrobe lies on its side, blocking the path.
- **[Crouch]** — The player crawls under the tilted wardrobe.
- **[Prone]** — A section where the ceiling has dropped to waist height. The player goes prone and crawls through, concrete scraping overhead.

*[ACTING NOTE: The building is a character. It speaks in groans and shifts. Every surface is provisional. Sami is trusting structures he would know, as an engineer, are unsafe. His expertise tells him this building could come down. He's in it anyway.]*

**Sound:** Sami's breathing, close and amplified. The scrape of his body on concrete. The building's low complaint.

**Floor 3 → Floor 4:**
- The stairwell between 3 and 4 is gone — a hole where the stairs were. But the landing on floor 4 is visible, two metres above.
- **[Jump + Climb]** — The player jumps to catch the edge of the landing, then pulls up. A piece of the edge breaks away — Sami scrambles, catches a door frame, hauls himself up.
- **Sound:** The chunk of concrete falling. A crash below. Then silence. Then the building groans again.

**Floor 4:**
- The hallway is intact but dark. The flashlight beam reveals graffiti — not revolutionary art but something older: a child's height markings on a door frame, with dates. 2009. 2010. 2011. The markings stop.

**[INTERACTION — HEIGHT MARKINGS]**

> **Arabic:** خطوط طول ع باب. ولد أو بنت — كل سنة خط جديد. ٢٠٠٩. ٢٠١٠. ٢٠١١. ما في ٢٠١٢.
> **English:** Height markings on a door frame. A boy or girl — a new line each year. 2009. 2010. 2011. There is no 2012.

**Floor 4 → Floor 5:**
- This stairwell is partially intact. The player climbs, testing each step. One step gives way — the player stumbles, catches themselves.
- At the top of floor 5: a hole in the exterior wall, large enough to step through. Beyond it — moonlight. The rooftops. And below, directly below: the no-man's-land street.

### Scene 3B-2: The View from Above

**Location:** Fifth floor, exterior wall opening
**Time:** 9:00 PM
**Lighting:** Moonlight. The street below is silver-grey. The regime checkpoint is visible to the west, its lamp a yellow point. To the east, the dark mass of Ghouta. And below — about fifteen metres down — the shape on the road. The blanket. Khaled.
**Ambient sound:** Wind at height. The building swaying almost imperceptibly. The open sky.

*Sami looks down. From here, the shape is just a shape. Small. Still. The blanket has shifted — one corner has blown back. He can see — he pulls his eyes away. He does not let himself see.*

**[INTERACTION — THE VIEW]**

> **Arabic:** من هون بشوف الشارع كلو. الحاجز، النور، الحرام يلي عالأرض. ومن هون — بشوف يلي شافو القنّاص. شارع فاضي وشخص عم يمشي. من هالعلو — كل إنسان بيصير نقطة.
> **English:** From here I can see the whole street. The checkpoint, the light, the blanket on the ground. And from here — I see what the sniper saw. An empty street and a person walking. From this height — every person becomes a dot.

*[ACTING NOTE: This is "The Sniper's View" from the suggested moments. The vertigo is not just physical. Sami is standing where a man with a rifle would stand. He sees what that man saw. The dehumanisation of distance — how a person becomes a target becomes a shape — is visible from here. It should make the player nauseous.]*

### Gameplay — The Descent

*The descent is harder. Sami must find a way down to street level on the eastern side of the building — away from the checkpoint's view. He climbs down external walls, drops between balconies, uses a dangling piece of fire-escape ladder.*

**[GAMEPLAY SEQUENCE]**
- **Crouch** along a fifth-floor balcony
- **Jump** to the balcony below (fourth floor) — a two-metre drop
- **Climb** down a drainpipe — the pipe creaks, shifts, but holds
- **Prone** through a ground-floor window into an alley

*Sami reaches street level. He is behind the buildings, in shadow, a few metres from the edge of the no-man's-land. The shape under the blanket is close now — twenty metres.*

**[GAMEPLAY — CRAWLING TO THE BODY]**

*The player goes prone and crawls. Slowly. The moonlight is bright — exposure risk. The checkpoint is visible but Sami is in the shadow of the buildings. He crawls across gravel, glass, debris. The sound is agonisingly loud in the silence.*

*He reaches the blanket. He does not lift it. He takes the edges and, still prone, begins to pull. Slowly. Dragging the covered form back toward the shadows.*

*[ACTING NOTE: The player never sees Khaled's body. The blanket stays on. What the player sees is the weight — the resistance of a human body being dragged across gravel, the sound it makes, the effort it requires. The physical reality of death without the visual.]*

**Sound:** The scrape of the blanket on the road. Sami's breathing, ragged. The crunch of gravel. Nothing from the checkpoint — or everything from the checkpoint, depending on whether they hear.

*Sami pulls the covered form into the shadow of the building. He sits. He's breathing hard. His hands are shaking. The blanket is in front of him, lumped and still.*

*He rests a hand on it. One hand, on the fabric, where the shoulder would be.*

**Sound:** Silence. Then crickets. Then Sami's breathing, slowly steadying.

## PART 3: THE NIGHT WALK — المشي بالليل

*[ALL PATHS — the following scenes are experienced by all players, in different orders depending on path. Players on the retrieval path reach them after the negotiation or building sequence. Witness and grief path players reach them as they walk through the night.]*

### Scene 3C-1: Moonlight Streets

**Location:** Various streets — the town at night
**Time:** 10:00 PM – midnight (varies)
**Lighting:** Full moonlight — three-quarter moon, high now. The town is transformed. The damaged buildings are silver sculptures. Shadows are sharp and black. The grape vines that climb through rubble catch the light on their leaves. There is a beauty to it that feels like betrayal — as though the moonlight is mocking the daylight's horror by making everything look clean.
**Ambient sound:** Crickets, dominant. The occasional distant shot — single, almost ceremonial in the quiet. The creak and settle of buildings. A dog, far away. Sami's footsteps, the only nearby human sound.

*[ACTING NOTE: The night walk is the game's contemplative core. The pace should be slow. The player walks through a town made unfamiliar by moonlight and grief. There are no enemies, no urgency, no objectives for several minutes. The player simply walks. The game trusts the player to feel this.]*

**[GAMEPLAY — FREE WALK]**

*The player moves through the town. Several interaction points and set-piece moments are available along the route. Some are optional, some are triggered by proximity.*

**[INTERACTION — BOMBED BUILDING AT NIGHT]**

> **English:** At night the bombed building becomes something else. The moon fills the open rooms with light. Like ghost houses — inhabited but not occupied.

### Scene 3C-2: The Cat Returns

**Location:** A narrow alley between two buildings
**Time:** ~10:30 PM

*Sami walks through a narrow alley. Ahead, on a low wall, a shape. The flashlight catches it: the **cat**. The same tabby, torn ear, amber eyes. It's sitting exactly as before — still, watching.*

*The cat looks at Sami. Sami stops. They regard each other.*

*The cat is thinner than before, if that's possible. Its ribs are sharp ridges under the fur. But its eyes are alive — attentive, present.*

*Sami crouches. He reaches into his pocket — nothing. He has no food. The cat knows this. It doesn't expect anything.*

*They sit together for a moment. Two breathing creatures in the dark. The moonlight falls between them.*

*The cat yawns — a wide, unselfconscious yawn that shows every tooth. Then it settles, tucking its paws under its chest. It closes its eyes.*

*Sami watches it. Then he stands, slowly, and walks on. The cat stays, curled, sleeping or pretending to.*

*[ACTING NOTE: The cat's yawn is the most peaceful moment in the game. It's a creature doing a creature thing — unburdened by grief, politics, history, siege. The yawn is not symbolic. It is a yawn. Its effect on Sami is precisely that it carries no meaning. In a night saturated with meaning, the meaninglessness is relief.]*

**Sound:** The cat's quiet purring — barely audible — fading as Sami walks away.

### Scene 3C-3: The Wedding

**Location:** Near a basement entrance — a residential building
**Time:** ~11:00 PM

**[INTERACTION — BASEMENT DOOR]**

> **Arabic:** باب قبو. ورا الباب — موسيقى. عرس. ناس عم تتزوّج وسط الحصار. الحياة عم تصر على حالها.
> **English:** A basement door. Behind it — music. A wedding. People getting married in the middle of a siege. Life insisting on itself.

A young woman in a white dress made from curtain material. A young man beside her, nervous, grinning. An old man playing oud in the corner. Children dancing. Adults clapping.*

*The music washes over Sami. He stands in the doorway, not entering, watching through the gap.*

*A woman near the door sees him. She doesn't recognise him, but she smiles and gestures — come in. The universal gesture of welcome at a celebration.*

*Sami shakes his head, gently. He closes the door. The music muffles behind it.*

*[ACTING NOTE: Sami doesn't go in because his grief would be wrong in that room. Not unwelcome — just wrong, the wrong frequency. The wedding is real joy, real defiance. His presence would contaminate it with what he carries. He protects their joy by staying outside. But he lets the music touch him.]*

**Sound:** The muffled music continues as Sami walks away. It fades slowly over ten seconds.

## PART 4: THE VISIONS — الرؤى

*[ALL PATHS — triggered in the small hours when exhaustion sets in]*

### Scene 3D-1: The Classroom Vision

**Location:** Transition from street to Khaled's basement classroom
**Time:** ~1:00 AM
**Lighting:** Sami is walking. The moonlit street begins to shift — the colours desaturate, then hypersaturate. The shadows deepen. The ambient sound warps, pulling into a low hum.

**[VISION — VISUAL SHIFT]**

*The street dissolves. Sami is standing in the doorway of a room: Khaled's basement classroom. But it's full. Every desk is occupied. The candles are lit. The blackboard has writing on it — Khaled's handwriting. An Arabic conjugation table.*

*But the students are wrong. They're adults — faces Sami recognises. People from the neighbourhood. People who died during the siege. The old man from the well — if the player didn't help him, he's here. A young man who was killed in a mortar strike months ago. A teenage girl from the next street. They sit at children's desks, their adult bodies crammed into the small chairs, and they write in exercise books with the concentration of eight-year-olds.*

*At the board, **Khaled** stands. He is writing. He is whole, alive, present. The candlelight is warm on his face. He turns.*

**خالد:**
> *(smiling)* تأخّرت، يا هندسة.
> *(smiling)* You're late, ya handasa.

*Sami tries to speak. No sound comes.*

**خالد:**
> *(turning back to the board, writing)* ماضي... حاضر... مستقبل.
> *(turning back to the board, writing)* Past... present... future.

*He turns again. His expression is different — not the grin from Act 1, but something quieter, more knowing. He looks at Sami the way you look at someone across a great distance.*

**خالد:**
> خلّص الدرس يا هندسة.
> *Finish the lesson, ya handasa.*

*Sami blinks. The candles gutter. The students' faces blur. The room empties — the desks are overturned, the board is smeared, the candles are out. Dust. Silence. The room is as it actually is: abandoned, dark, cold.*

*Sami stands in the doorway, flashlight in hand, staring at an empty room.*

**Sound:** The silence after a vision. Then: his own heartbeat. Then: the crickets reasserting themselves.

*[ACTING NOTE: The vision is not supernatural. It's a grieving, exhausted mind filling an empty room with what it needs. The player should feel the warmth of the full classroom, then the brutal cold of its emptiness. Khaled's last words — "Finish the lesson" — are not a command but an echo, the kind of thing the mind produces when it doesn't know what else to do with loss.]*

---

### Scene 3D-2: The Orchard Vision

**Location:** Transition from street to the grape orchards outside town
**Time:** ~1:45 AM

*Sami is walking. He passes through an archway — and the world shifts. He is outdoors, but not in the town. He is in the **grape orchards** that ring Ghouta — the orchards that were once famous, that supplied Damascus with fruit.*

**[VISION — VISUAL SHIFT]**

*The orchards are intact. No shelling damage. No craters. Row after row of vines, heavy with ripe purple grapes. The moonlight is replaced by golden sunlight — late summer afternoon. The air is warm and smells of earth and ripe fruit. An irrigation channel runs alongside the path, water flowing clear and cold.*

*Sami walks through the orchard. Grape leaves brush his arms. The fruit hangs low. He reaches out to pick a grape—*

*His hand passes through it. The grape is there but not there. Visible but intangible. He tries again. The same. The orchard is a photograph, not a place.*

*In the distance, a figure walks between the rows. It's Khaled — small, far away, moving away from Sami. Sami walks faster. Khaled doesn't get closer. He doesn't turn around.*

*The sunlight begins to dim. The vines darken. The water in the channel slows, thickens, stops. The grapes shrivel — not slowly but all at once, contracting to raisins on the vine. The leaves curl and brown. The orchard dies in seconds.*

*Sami stands in the ruined present: a cratered field on the town's edge, the remains of a vine, the irrigation channel dry and filled with rubble. Moonlight.*

**Sound:** Through the vision: birdsong, water, wind through leaves. At the dissolution: a deep silence, then the present's ambient returns — distant artillery, crickets, wind over empty ground.

### Scene 3D-3: The Damascus Vision

**Location:** Transition from street to a Damascus boulevard
**Time:** ~2:15 AM

*Sami is walking past a building. In a shop window — cracked, empty — he sees a reflection that isn't his. A younger version of himself, in clean clothes, carrying a university bag. He turns—*

**[VISION — VISUAL SHIFT]**

*He is on a Damascus street. The real Damascus — the one from before. Traffic. Pedestrians. Shop windows full of things. A man selling falafel. A woman talking on a mobile phone. A bus passing. The noise of a city that works.*

*Sami is invisible. He walks among the people and they don't see him. He sees himself — twenty-two, in jeans and a polo shirt, crossing the road. His younger self has earphones in and is half-smiling at something on his phone.*

*Sami tries to call out. Nothing. He reaches out to touch his younger self's shoulder. His hand passes through.*

*Young Sami walks into a café. Through the window: he sits down across from young Khaled. They laugh. Khaled talks with his hands — always with his hands. Young Sami stirs sugar into coffee. The afternoon light is golden through the window.*

*Present Sami watches through the glass. He puts his hand on the window. It's cold.*

**Sound:** The city sounds are slightly out of tune — a half-step off, like a record at wrong speed. As the vision dissolves, the sounds slow and distort.

*The café, the street, the traffic — all contract, folding inward. The window becomes a wall. The wall is damaged. Sami's hand is on cold concrete. He's in the town. It's 2:15 AM. Damascus is five kilometres away and might as well be on another planet.*

## PART 5: THE FLASHBACKS — الذكريات

### Scene 3E-1: Eid Before the Revolution

**Location:** Memory — a family home, probably 2008 or 2009
**Time:** Triggered by passing a shuttered doorway

**[FLASHBACK — VISUAL SHIFT]**

*A room full of people. A family gathering — Eid al-Fitr. The table is covered: lamb shoulder, rice, kibbeh, tabbouleh, bowls of yoghurt, flatbreads stacked. Fifteen people around a table meant for eight. Children on the floor. Someone has put a cloth over the television to stop the children from watching instead of eating.*

*Sami is maybe fifteen. He sits between his mother and an uncle. His father is at the head of the table, carving the lamb. The room is loud — three conversations at once, a grandmother complaining about the temperature of the rice, two cousins arguing about football.*

**سامي الأب / Sami's Father:**
> *(to the table, raising a glass of juice)* كل عام وإنتو بخير.
> *(to the table, raising a glass of juice)* Happy Eid, everyone.

**الكل / Everyone:**
> وإنت بخير!
> *And you!*

*They eat. The noise is the noise of a family that is safe and fed and together. It's so ordinary it shouldn't be memorable. But Sami remembers it because of what happens next.*

*A **knock at the door**.*

*The room goes silent. Instantly. Fifteen people, mid-bite, mid-sentence — silent. Sami's father puts down his knife. His mother's hand finds Sami's arm under the table and grips it.*

*The silence lasts three seconds. It's the longest three seconds of Sami's childhood. In those three seconds, every person at the table is thinking the same thing: the mukhabarat.*

*Sami's father goes to the door. He opens it. It's the **neighbour** — Abu Hisham, carrying a tray of baklava. His wife made too much, he says. Happy Eid.*

*The room exhales. Conversations resume. Someone laughs. The uncle pours more juice. The lamb gets carved.*

*But Sami remembers his mother's hand on his arm. The grip. The three seconds. The way the room stopped breathing.*

*[ACTING NOTE: This is the unspoken terror. Not a dramatic event — a pause. A knock. A silence. The entire Assad era lived in moments like this: the constant, low-frequency fear that the state could arrive at any door, at any time, and there was nothing you could do. The family never discusses the silence. It never needs discussing. Everyone understood.]*

**[END FLASHBACK]**

---

### Scene 3E-2: The First Protest

**Location:** Memory — a street after Friday prayers, 2011
**Time:** Triggered by passing the cracked mosque minaret

**[FLASHBACK — VISUAL SHIFT]**

*A Friday. March 2011. The air is different — charged, electric. People are pouring out of the mosque. More people than usual. And they're not going home.*

*Sami is twenty-four. Khaled is beside him. They're at the edge of the crowd — not committed yet, watching. The crowd begins to move, flowing down the main street. Someone starts a chant.*

**الحشد / The Crowd:**
> *(chanting)* الشعب يريد إسقاط النظام!
> *(chanting)* The people want the fall of the regime!

*The chant builds. It echoes off the buildings. People come to windows, to balconies. Some join from the side streets. The crowd swells.*

**خالد:**
> *(to Sami, eyes wide, voice shaking with something between fear and exhilaration)* يلا.
> *(to Sami, eyes wide, voice shaking with something between fear and exhilaration)* Let's go.

*They step into the current. The crowd absorbs them. Sami's mouth moves — he's chanting, he realises. He's chanting. Words he has never said aloud. Words that were punishable by imprisonment. They're pouring out of him.*

*Khaled is grinning — the same grin from Act 1, the same full, unguarded face. But younger, raw, terrified and alive.*

*Then — gunfire. Far at first, then closer. The crowd falters, sways, then scatters. Chaos. Running. Screaming. Sami grabs Khaled's sleeve and they run — down an alley, around a corner, behind a dumpster. They press against the metal, breathing hard.*

**Sound:** Gunfire receding. Footsteps running. Someone screaming. Then: heavy breathing. Two young men behind a dumpster.

**خالد:**
> *(breathing hard, but grinning — grinning)* ما رح يرجعوا يسكتونا.
> *(breathing hard, but grinning — grinning)* They're never going to silence us again.

*Sami looks at him. Khaled's face in the alley light, flushed, electric, alive. This is the face Sami will carry for the rest of his life.*

**[END FLASHBACK]**

*The present: the mosque's cracked minaret in moonlight. The street is empty. The crowd is gone. The chanting is gone. The revolution became a war became a siege and the people who wanted the fall of the regime are now trying to survive it. Khaled is gone. The grin is gone.*

### Scene 3E-3: The Mukhabarat

**Location:** Memory — a school classroom, ~2001
**Time:** Triggered by the flashlight illuminating a portrait on a wall

*Sami's flashlight beam catches something on a wall inside a damaged building: a **portrait**. Half-burned, water-damaged, but recognisable. The standard official portrait that hung in every classroom, every office, every shop.*

**[FLASHBACK — VISUAL SHIFT]**

*A school classroom. Sami is ten. Rows of desks. A teacher at the front. The portrait on the wall above the blackboard, flanked by a Syrian flag. The teacher is reciting something — Ba'athist party slogans, required curriculum.*

*Sami sits next to a boy — **Fadi** (not the battery Fadi from Act 1 — a different Fadi, a childhood friend). Fadi passes Sami a note under the desk. Sami unfolds it. It's a joke — a joke about the portrait. Sami stifles a laugh.*

*The teacher sees. He walks to Sami's desk. He takes the note. He reads it. His face empties.*

**المعلم / The Teacher:**
> *(controlled)* مين كتب هاد؟
> *(controlled)* Who wrote this?

*Silence. Fadi stares at his desk.*

**المعلم:**
> *(leaning close to Sami, whispering)* اطوي هالورقة. حطّها بجيبتك. اوعى تحكي عنها لحدا. ما كانت هون. ما شفتها. فهمت؟
> *(leaning close to Sami, whispering)* Fold this paper. Put it in your pocket. Don't tell anyone about it. It was never here. I never saw it. Understand?

*Sami nods, terrified. The teacher returns to the front. He resumes the lesson as if nothing happened.*

*[ACTING NOTE: The teacher protected them. In that moment, the teacher could have reported the note — and both Sami and Fadi and possibly their families would have been visited by the mukhabarat. Instead, he chose not to see. That choice — the choice to look away, to pretend, to protect through silence — is the Assad era's defining moral act. Millions of small, daily decisions to not see, not hear, not speak. Some of them were acts of complicity. Some were acts of kindness. Often they were both at once.]*

*Sami goes home that afternoon. His father is at the door. He knows — somehow, parents always knew. He takes Sami into the kitchen, closes the door.*

**سامي الأب:**
> *(very quietly, very seriously)* شو ما بتسمع بالبيت، ما بتحكي عنو بالمدرسة. وشو ما بتسمع بالمدرسة — بتنسى. فهمت؟
> *(very quietly, very seriously)* Whatever you hear at home, you don't talk about at school. And whatever you hear at school — you forget. Understood?

*Ten-year-old Sami nods.*

**سامي الأب:**
> مش لأنو غلط تحكي. لأنو في ناس بتعاقب الناس يلي بتحكي.
> *Not because it's wrong to speak. Because there are people who punish the people who speak.*

**[END FLASHBACK]**

*Sami stands in the ruined building, flashlight on the burned portrait. The face in the portrait stares out, serene and absolute. Sami lowers the flashlight. The portrait disappears into darkness.*

## PART 6: THE ROOFTOP — السطح

*[ALL PATHS — the final shared scene of Act 3]*

### Scene 3F-1: The Climb

**Location:** A six-storey building in the eastern quarter, accessible by intact stairs
**Time:** 2:30 AM
**Lighting:** Deep night. The moon has moved across the sky and is now in the west, lower, the light more angled. Stars — visible, thick, the Milky Way clear as it only is when there are no city lights.
**Ambient sound:** Wind at height. The town below is nearly silent. A dog. The distant sound of a generator — someone running one for a medical need, an exception to the dark.

*Sami climbs to the rooftop. The stairs are intact — this building is one of the less damaged. The stairwell is dark; he cranks the flashlight.*

*He emerges onto the roof. The view opens.*

### Scene 3F-2: The View

**Location:** The rooftop
**Time:** 2:35 AM
**Lighting:** The sky is immense. To the west, Damascus: a constellation of electric light, orange and white and blue, stretched across the horizon. Highways, buildings, streetlights. The city glows. To the east, north, south: Ghouta. Dark. A few isolated points of light — candles, a generator — but overwhelmingly dark. The siege made visible: a city of light and a ring of darkness around it, five kilometres apart, separated by checkpoints, snipers, and the decision to forget.

**Ambient sound:** Wind. The hum of Damascus — barely audible, carried on the air. A car horn, impossibly faint. The sound of a city that works, arriving from a distance that might as well be space.

*[ACTING NOTE: The player should be given time here. This is not a gameplay moment. It is a looking moment. The view says what no dialogue can: that normalcy exists five kilometres away and these people have been cut off from it. Damascus is there, lit up, alive, continuing. Someone in Damascus is watching television right now. Someone is eating dinner. Someone is complaining about traffic. And here, in the dark ring around it, people are starving, and being bombed, and burying their friends in the grape orchards.]*

**[GAMEPLAY — NO TIMER]**

*The player can stay on the rooftop as long as they want. There is no objective, no prompt, no countdown. The wind blows. The lights of Damascus twinkle. The dark of Ghouta surrounds them.*

**[INTERACTION — GHOUTA]**

> **Arabic:** الغوطة. عتمة من كل الجهات. نقاط نور — شمعة هون، مولّد هونيك — بس أغلبها عتمة. من هون بتشوف الحصار بعيون طير.
> **English:** Ghouta. Darkness in every direction. Points of light — a candle here, a generator there — but mostly dark. From here you see the siege through a bird's eyes.

*After the player has spent time on the rooftop — a minimum of fifteen seconds of real time — a **sound** rises from somewhere below. The fajr azan, the dawn call to prayer, from a mosque with no electricity and no loudspeaker — a man's voice, unaccompanied, carrying on the wind.*

**Sound:** The azan begins. A single voice, trembling, resonant, calling from a rooftop or a minaret somewhere in the dark town. "الله أكبر... الله أكبر..." The voice cracks slightly on the high note. It is imperfect and it is the most human sound in the game.

*The sky to the east begins to change — the deepest blue lightening, infinitely slowly, at the horizon. The first suggestion of dawn.*

### Key Emotional Beats
1. **The Crossing** — walking sixty metres in moonlight under the white cloth, utterly exposed
2. **Maher's photograph** — two girls in a garden, the human face of complicity
3. **The Sniper's View** — seeing what the sniper saw, the dehumanisation of distance
4. **The Cat's Yawn** — a moment of absolute, meaningless peace
5. **The Wedding** — joy continuing despite everything, and Sami choosing not to enter
6. **The Classroom Vision** — "You're late, ya handasa"
7. **The Eid Knock** — the three-second silence, the Assad era in miniature
8. **The First Protest** — Khaled grinning: "They're never going to silence us again"
9. **The Father's Warning** — "Not because it's wrong to speak"
10. **The Rooftop** — Damascus lit, Ghouta dark, five kilometres of forgetting

### Music / Score Notes
- **The Crossing:** No score. Only footsteps, breathing, cloth, wind. The silence does the work.
- **The Negotiation:** No score. The radio crackle and lamp hum are the only texture.
- **The Building:** Low tension: a sustained bass drone, building with each floor. Drops to silence at the top.
- **Night Walk:** A single ney line, very quiet, threading through the ambient sound. Not a melody — a breath. It comes and goes.
- **Visions:** Each vision has its own sound world: the classroom has warm ambient hum; the orchard has birdsong and water; Damascus has city noise — all slightly distorted, slightly wrong.
- **Flashbacks:** Diegetic only — family noise, crowd chanting, school ambience. No underscore.
- **The Rooftop:** Wind. Nothing else. Until the azan, which is the only unaccompanied human voice in the score.
